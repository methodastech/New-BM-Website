Chapter I

The Game

A horror condensed from grief, an endless descent, and one engine with two doors.

You are a horror condensed from a field of grief. Below the meadow lies the Bonefield, a dungeon of endless layers. You descend, consume the dead and the living to take their power, and climb back to walk among humans wearing a stolen face.

The more you kill and hoard, the higher your bounty, and the deadlier the hunters the kingdom sends after you. You learn by devouring; you fight with steel in one hand and incantations on your tongue; and the only way is down.

The 12-second pitch

A Vampire-Survivors-meets-Sekiro action-roguelite where you cast spells by speaking the incantation aloud. Swing a sword like a knight, or chant for two minutes to unleash an apocalypse. Mispronounce the words and the spell fizzles, weakens, or detonates in your own face. Born of sorrow, you descend an endless dungeon, eating power and hiding among the people who hunt you.

05Two clocks at once

The loop above tells you what you do. This is how it feels to do it. Bonefield runs two clocks at once: one ticks in seconds, one across a whole run, and learning both is what turns a button-press into dread.

The Fast Clock

Speak under pressure

A blade never misses for nerves; a spell can. Casting asks you to hold a word steady while a knight winds up, so every incantation is a small bet that you finish before you are hit. The fast clock runs two to five seconds.

The Two Bars

Stamina against essence

Blade work spends stamina and keeps you safe. Spells spend essence and rewrite the fight. Run either dry at the wrong moment and you hold the weaker tool against the deadlier foe. Two bars, two ways to die.

The Gamble

The cost of greed

Hold a word longer to charge a higher tier, and a higher tier is a longer window of exposure. Release early for a sure, smaller hit, or hold for the devastating one and pray nothing reaches you first. Safe at D, devastating at S.

The slow clock is the run itself: a quiet descent that swells into a hunt as your bounty climbs, a boss or an escape near the brink, then death, which is not loss but deposit. Banked essence, forged spells and known forms carry into the next descent, so every ending begins the next run stronger. The fast clock teaches you in seconds; the slow clock is what makes you whisper "one more layer."

Chapter II

The Story

A slime with a mind and an appetite wakes in a mass grave, and the Kingdom above learns to fear what it buried.

The meadow looks like any other. Green grass, a low wind, a sky the colour of old milk. Beneath it lies the dead of every losing war, dumped in trenches and forgotten under flowers. Grief soaked the soil for a hundred years until it could no longer stay buried. It thickened. It cooled into a single will. You woke in that mass grave as a slime with a mind and an appetite, made of everything the living left behind. You are not their ghost. You are what their suffering became when there was finally enough of it.

I remember every mouth that screamed into this dirt, and none of them are mine. I keep them anyway. The dead taught me hunger before they taught me my name, so I ate first and learned after. I am still learning. There is so much down here to become.

The slime, first waking beneath the meadow

01Who you are

Origin

Born of grief

A century of slaughter pooled in one field and refused to rest. Sorrow condensed past the point of feeling into a thing that moves and wants. You are the residue of every death this ground swallowed, given a shape and a purpose it never asked for.

Nature

Essence-engineer

You do not pray to power or pay for it. You take it apart. Every corpse and every living thing carries essence, and you read it the way a smith reads metal, then forge it into forms, spells, and the raising of the dead. Consumption is your craft.

Truth

A horror

You wear sympathy like a stolen coat, yet underneath you are appetite with a memory. You eat the dead, then the dying, then whoever is left. The Kingdom is right to fear you. The kindest thing said of you above will be a lie you told first.

The world

Five names that shape every run.

  • The Graveyard Meadow. The pleasant green field where you woke, a mass grave dressed as a pasture. Its flowers feed on the dead, and so do you. It is the mouth of the descent and the first thing you will learn to leave behind.
  • The Bonefield. The endless layered dungeon under the meadow, dug by no one and going down forever. Each layer holds older dead and stronger foes, and the essence grows richer the further you fall. This is the descent itself, your hunting ground and your hourglass.
  • The Essence Pool. Your banked store, the core resource you carry between forms and runs. It buys shapes, spells, and the rite of raising the dead. Spend it wisely, because everything you become is paid out of this single well.
  • The Surface. The world above the gate, full of villages, roads, and watching eyes. You walk it in a stolen body to gather, listen, and decide who learns what you are. It is calm in the way a held breath is calm.
  • The Kingdom. The realm that owns the gate and the gallows, ruled by a Crown that prices monsters by the head. It marches soldiers, then knights, then a commander when your bounty climbs too high. It is the weight that answers everything you take.

02The cast

The Crown

The Lord-Commander

When your bounty peaks, the Crown stops sending boys with spears and sends him. He marches from the dungeon gate at the head of a column, banner first, and he has buried things like you before. He does not negotiate, and he does not break.

The Choir

The Inquisitor

The Choir gave him a name for you long before you had one of your own. Abomination, written in the registers, read aloud at the gate. He hunts your stolen faces with relic and rite, and the common folk love him because he promises them you can be unmade.

The Folk

The Villager

A frightened face at a half-shut door, the kind of body you might wear or the kind of life you might spare. Whether you take the face or leave it shapes what the Common Folk whisper of you, and whether the next door opens or bars from inside.

The Underworld

The Courtier of the Dead

In the deep courts, the Underworld keeps its own etiquette, and he keeps the ledger. He trades in essence the way nobles trade in land, and he will deal with you if your standing is high enough. He is not your friend. He simply finds you useful, for now.

The Mask

The Stolen Face

The body you wear above, taken from someone who no longer needs it. It walks, it talks, it answers to a name that was never yours. Hold it well and the town sees a neighbour. Let it slip and they see the seam, and behind the seam, you.

The Source

The First Grief

Not a person, the field itself, the oldest sorrow in the trench that woke before the rest. It is the shadow at your origin, the voice you sometimes mistake for memory. It wants what you want, deeper and colder, and it is always one layer below.

03The double life

The gate opens both ways. You climb out of the Bonefield wearing a face that breathed yesterday, and you walk into a town that buys your bread and your lies in the same breath. Up here you are not a horror. You are a quiet stranger with a name, an errand, and somewhere to be by dark.

The surface is where you gather what the deep cannot give. You listen for which patrol is short a man, which family will not be missed, which merchant sells without asking. Reputation rides with you into every street, so a town that fears your true shape will sell to your borrowed one until it learns the difference.

Then you go back down, because you always go back down. The mask is only a way to keep the field a secret a little longer.

The mask has a seam. Feed where you can be seen, let your shape slip, or arrive trailing a monstrous name, and the town begins to sense it. The Hunt names the price.

Chapter III

Spells & Casting

The heart of Bonefield, and the thing nothing else does: magic you perform with your own voice.

You do not press a button to cast. You speak, you shape, and one day you sign. Magic is performed, invented, and risked. Your pronunciation is your power.

Hold the cast key, speak the incantation aloud, and let go on the tier you want. Both hands stay free to move and dodge while your voice does the spellwork. The voice is the trigger, not a search bar.

01Four ways to cast

Mode I · Patterns

Shape

Draw a sigil with the cursor. Fast and quiet, but Level-1 only. The warrior's quick-cast: a flick of fire mid-swing.

Mode II · Incantation

Voice

Chant the words aloud. Each correct phrase charges a higher tier. Release with a command word. The mage's true power lives here.

Mode III · Gesture

Camera

Hand-sign the spell to the webcam. Planned, honest "coming soon" (needs hand-tracking). The third school of casting.

Mode IV · Rite

Summon

You speak the dead one's name or epithet and the Rite of Raising reforms the corpse you devoured into a thrall bound to your will. This is the necromancer's school, where banked essence is spent to call a host that rots on a timer and collapses back to a sip you reclaim.

02The sigils, as drawn

No mic. A loud room. A silent night with a sleeping house. When the voice will not serve you, draw the word instead. Hold the cast key, trace the shape with your hand, and the field reads the line you left behind. Same spells, same tiers, no loss of depth: only the medium changes. The recogniser counts your reversals and your closure: a wrong shape fizzles and stings.

How the field reads a line

Hold E and drag. The reader wants at least eight points, a stroke ninety pixels long, then it counts two things: how many times your hand doubled back, and whether your line came home to close. Draw the wrong word and it fizzles in your grip: a small sting, and the mana is gone. Blade when they reach you. Word when they run.

The pronunciation mechanic

The signature of the whole game. Say it aloud in the prototype and the field listens, scores you, and answers in kind.

  • Accuracy drives power. Say it clean, hit harder. Slur it and the spell weakens.
  • Mistakes punish. "Fireball" said as "firebaxxl", a couple of wrong syllables, and the spell loses power, fizzles, or backfires and detonates in your own face.
  • Risk scales with reward. The longest, most powerful chants are the easiest to ruin, and leave you defenceless while you speak.
  • Master it for real. Players literally get better at saying the words. The skill is yours, not the character's.

04The voice, verbatim

The prototype does not guess at your intent: it listens for real words, in a real grammar. An element word chooses the school. A power word deepens the charge. A release word lets it fly. This is the whole tongue the field understands today, written out plainly, exactly as it hears it.

Speak them aloud in the prototype: press V, then E, and the field listens for eight seconds. Your pronunciation is your power; mispronounce the words and the spell fizzles, weakens, or detonates in your own face.

06The Forge of Spells

Casting "fireball" is the beginning, not the spell. Bonefield lets you invent magic the way Minecraft lets you build: from parts, by combination, into something nobody else has made, and made to be shared.

Every crafted spell is the sum of six dials: turn one and the spell, its damage, and how it looks all change. Its power is decided by word choice, combination, and voice: which spirits you name, whether they gel or clash, and how cleanly you speak. The forge gels it into one spell, its damage, its behaviour.

Dial I

Spirits

Which elements you invoke, and in what ratio. Fire · water · earth · metal · wind · thunder · light · death · nature.

Dial II

Power

How long and how cleanly you chant. More words, more force. Flick → charged → apocalyptic.

Dial III

Delivery

How it leaves you and reaches them. Thrown · held · beam · floating mine (triggers on touch) · aura · written sigil.

Dial IV

Force

From a gentle nudge to a violent hurl. Soft push ↔ hard slam.

Dial V

Form

Shape, colour, how it erupts from you, how it looks on the kill. Bolt · orb · spear · spray · dot · bloom.

Dial VI

Stability

Safe, unstable, or forbidden. The deadliest spells can take your own life. Stable · volatile · forbidden.

Chapter IV

The Hunt

Power is taken, and taking is noticed: the world answers in Fear, Bounty, and Reputation, and you answer back with a stolen face and a marching horde.

Power is taken, and taking is noticed. Every corpse you fold into yourself, every coin you hoard, every face you steal leaves a mark the world can read. So the world answers, never in one voice but in registers. The creatures beside you answer in Fear, the dread they feel as you draw near. The Crown answers in Bounty and Heat, the price it sets on your ruin within a single descent. Every faction answers in Reputation, the long memory that outlasts your deaths and decides who shelters you or draws steel. You answer back in kind. You raise the dead you have eaten into a marching Horde, and you wear a borrowed body on the surface, holding a disguise no longer than its Suspicion allows.

These are not separate meters but one connected web. Fear is the moment, Bounty the run, Reputation the saga, the Mask the seam that binds expectation to instinct, and the Rite of Raising the answer you give to all of them.

You are a horror. Your nearness breaks people down a ladder of dread, and broken enemies fight worse, flee, or beg for their lives. Calm holds ground, chatters, unaware of you. Anxious edges back, hands beginning to tremble. Afraid backs away steadily and calls for help. Terror breaks: screams, runs, or drops and begs.

And success is dangerous. Every kill and coin raises your bounty rank, and the kingdom answers with deadlier hunters. That is the tension that drives "one more layer." Push deeper for richer essence, or the elite come to collect.

01Reputation: how the world remembers you

Fear is a moment. It rises in the foe across the room and dies when the room is clear. Bounty is a single run: the Crown counts your kills and your hoarded gold and marches an answer from the dungeon gate. Reputation outlives both. It is a standing, kept separately by four factions, that survives your death and your descent. Where Fear and Bounty govern the fight below, Reputation governs the surface above: who shelters your stolen body, who sells to it, who spits a rumour of your true face, and who draws steel before you speak.

Each of the four keeps its own standing, and the marker is yours to move. The Crown hardens with every soldier you devour and cools when you spare a patrol or return stolen tribute. The Choir damns you fastest for raising the dead and wearing a stolen face, and its sermons soften for a feigned pilgrim who burns no shrines and leaves the relics alone. The Folk bar their doors when word spreads that the slaughter wears a human shape, and shelter you without asking when you clear a haunt and leave them coin. The Underworld respects strength and the rite of raising: honour a pact and feed the dead their due, and it trades rare essence and passage; cross it and the deep itself sets ambushes in your path. The needle starts against you: the Choir furthest into reviled, the Crown close behind, the Folk wary at the middle, and only the Underworld leaning toward revered.

Reputation persists. Death is a deposit, and your name travels with the deposit. Banked essence, forged spells, and known forms carry into the next descent, and so does every faction's memory of what you did to earn them. A run can spike Bounty and reset it; Reputation only moves the way you steered it.

The Mask: suspicion on the surface

When you walk the surface in a stolen body, the body carries a measure of how well the seam holds, called Suspicion. It is the seam binding Reputation to Fear: what a town expects of your face meets what its people sense in the moment, and Suspicion is the thread that pulls when the two no longer match.

The disguise climbs its own ladder. Settled: the town reads you as one of its own, doors open, prices hold. Watchful: eyes linger, sellers want a name, the watch notes where you go. Alarmed: trust thins to nothing, folk shutter early and send word to the garrison that something wears a false face. The Turn: the mask is named for what it is, the bell rings, and the Hunt begins at the threshold you stand on.

  • Feeding in sight. A witness to your hunger is a witness against your face, and Suspicion climbs with every pair of eyes. Feed in the dark, or leave no witness.
  • Letting the shape slip. The stolen body is a held form, not a true one. When it falters, a wrong joint, a wrong gait, a glimpse of the slime beneath, the seam shows and Suspicion rises. Hold the form, move as the dead one moved.
  • A monstrous name preceding you. Reputation arrives before you do. If the Crown brands you abomination or the Choir reads your face in a sermon, you enter the gate already half-suspect. Mend your standing before you walk in.

Past the threshold the town turns as one. Doors bar against you, the watch musters in the square, and your cover is spent. The Hunt no longer waits at the dungeon gate. It follows you up from below, into the streets where you thought yourself safe, and there is no walking out as a townsman now.

02Raise the Bonefield: summoning the dead

You do not bury what you swallow. You keep it, and when the field grows thick with foes you say the name again and the corpse stands up. You ate the dead, so you may raise them: spend banked essence to reform what you devoured into temporary thralls that march at your word, fight in your name, and rot on a timer until they collapse back to the dust you called them from.

This is where you stop fighting alone. Every build sits on a triangle of blade, word, and horde: the blade is your own strike, the word is the rite that calls and binds, and the horde is the standing army that turns a duel into a siege, with the Commander in the middle pulling from all three. A standing horde swells your Fear aura and raises your Bounty most of all, because the Kingdom dreads a risen army above any single monster. And every thrall rots from the instant it stands: when it collapses you reclaim a sip of the essence you spent to raise it, so a horde spent well feeds the next rite of raising rather than draining you dry.

Chapter V

The Shared Field

Co-op already runs: one of you hosts a world, the other steps into it, and only a four-rune word stands between you.

The descent was never yours alone, you only believed it was. The field that condensed you from grief condensed a thousand others, and they are below you now, beside you, ahead of you on the stair. Some hold the line at your shoulder. Some want the forms you wear. Some are only slow, and slowness on this ground is a kind of consent. You meet them in real time, and the meeting is never neutral: an ally, a rival, or prey too late to read what you are.

01Two of you, one truth

The Key

The four-rune word

The host mints a four-character room code and speaks it aloud. Your companion types those runes and steps through. One word opens the door, and no one who lacks it can find the seam.

The Truth

The host's world is true

The host's world is the true one: guests see mirrored foes drawn from it, and every spell you cast flies in every world at once. Two screens, one battlefield, no argument over what really happened.

What runs today

Co-op is in the build today, and experimental. It rides a peer-to-peer connection (PeerJS over WebRTC), so it needs the open internet but no server of ours. Host a room, read the four runes to a friend, and fight the field together.

  • Peer to peer, no keeper. Your machines speak straight to each other, serverless, with only the open internet between.
  • Your companion arrives whole. A full avatar with a floating name tag, fighting the field beside you as one.
  • Everything beyond this point is the design. Parties, tournaments, the one shared world: drawn plainly below, not yet playable.

02One world, many monsters

This is the design the co-op is a first taste of: one living world, no instances, no private copies. Every player a creature like you, and every one of you born the same.

The World

The shared world

One persistent map, surface and Bonefield both, ticking in real time. What another monster does here lands here: the bodies it leaves and the Bounty it raises are part of the world you walk into next. This is the horizon, not yet the build.

The Others

The others

Every player is a creature like you, learning by consuming, wearing stolen faces. You meet them as ally, as rival, or as prey, and which one they are is decided the instant you both reach for the same kill.

The Origin

The shared origin

Everyone begins as the same newborn slime, condensed from the graveyard of grief. Nothing is chosen at birth. What you become is earned in the descent, and the common start is the one story you all carry down.

You meet a thing on the road and one of you feeds the field. That is the only law the open world has ever kept.

On the open world to come

03The open world to come

There is no alignment menu, in the design. How you treat the world writes you, and the world reads it back.

The Deeds

Good or evil, by how you roam

The game watches who you spare and who you devour, who you guard and who you betray, and it sums your conduct into where you stand: Reviled, Feared, Watched, or Revered. That standing feeds your Reputation with every faction and decides how folk and monsters answer you when you arrive.

The Pack

Form a party

Alone you are one hunger. Banded together you are a pack: essence, gold, and the corpses worth raising are split among you, and so is every threat that turns to face you. The heavier the pack, the heavier the Crown's reply, and betrayal slides you toward the Reviled.

The Purse

Tournaments and street fights

The surface sanctions blood: enter the arena for coin and renown, climb a public ladder, and carry the purse back down into the dark. Away from the arena the world is open, and a fight is whatever finds you: a Crown patrol, a lone monster, a rival pack.

Five milestones to launch, two markets from one engine, and the honest, audited case for why it wins.

Open the one-page investor brief

04How we pay for it

Climb a ladder. Spend almost nothing to prove the hook, and let proof unlock the next rung. Only take on money once people have shown they want it. Debt before proof is the most common indie mistake.

  1. Step 0 · now

    Bootstrap

    Build the slice with what we already have. Cost: time plus near-free tools. Risk: none. Nobody funds a game they cannot play, so this comes first.

  2. Step 1 · free

    Prove it

    Ship the web demo and post clips. The single question that unlocks money: do casting clips get shared, and do wishlists climb? If yes, run a Kickstarter; proof is exactly what makes crowdfunding work.

  3. Step 2 · preferred

    Kickstarter

    Launch with a polished demo, a trailer, and a following. It funds 6 to 12 months of focused work, and the backers become the launch-day audience and reviewers. The best trade of risk and ownership.

  4. Step 3 · onward

    Self-sustaining

    Early Access and Steam sales repay any debt and fund the 1.0. From here the game pays for itself.

The Preferred Route

Kickstarter

No debt, you keep ownership, and backers become a built-in launch audience; it proves demand before you over-invest. The price: it needs a polished demo, a trailer and a following first, a miss is public, and fulfilment takes real time.

The Last Resort

A small family loan

Fast, flexible, no platform cut, patient terms over one to two years, no public risk. The price: personal relationships at stake, real debt with real repayment, and no marketing benefit, just cash. Written down, small, never plan A.

If we raise, where it goes. Lean by design: most of any raise buys focused dev time, not overhead.

07Why it wins: the audit

Not a wish, an audit. Here is the honest case for why this game can succeed, what already proves it, and where the proof still has to come from.

What we already know is public record: combination out-scales hand-built content; it is the engine under the biggest hits of the decade. People pay full price for a roguelite that feels great, and a tiny team can reach millions: Hades did, Vampire Survivors did. Browser voice recognition is now fast enough to drive an action game with no install. What we must still prove is ours to prove: that spoken combination feels as good as it reads on paper, that our moment-to-moment combat clears the quality bar, that the mic holds up in a loud living room, and that our specific clips travel, measured in wishlists.

Look closely at the biggest, longest-living hits of the last decade and one shape repeats. Hand players simple parts, let them combine those parts into outcomes the developers never authored, and make the results easy to show off. That is the engine under Minecraft. Bonefield runs the same engine, aimed at the action-roguelite that has never had it.
