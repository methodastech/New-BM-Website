The Game

You are a horror condensed from a field of grief. Below the meadow lies the Bonefield, a dungeon of endless layers. You descend, consume the dead and the living to take their power, and climb back to walk among humans wearing a stolen face.

The more you kill and hoard, the higher your bounty, and the deadlier the hunters the kingdom sends after you. You learn by devouring; you fight with steel in one hand and incantations on your tongue; and the only way is down.

The 12‑second pitch

A Vampire‑Survivors‑meets‑Sekiro action‑roguelite where you cast spells by speaking the incantation aloud. Swing a sword like a knight, or chant for two minutes to unleash an apocalypse. Mispronounce the words and the spell fizzles, weakens, or detonates in your own face. Born of sorrow, you descend an endless dungeon, eating power and hiding among the people who hunt you.

Who it is for

One engine, two doors. A dark game for adults, and a bright one for children who are still learning to speak.

Bonefield — for adults

The full descent: the story, the blood, the bounty, and multiplayer. You speak to cast, and you speak to survive. Mature by design, and honest about it.

The Flagship

Wordlight — for kids

The same voice magic with zero gore: a young apprentice learns real words by speaking spells. Children practice how to talk — pronounce, converse, negotiate — and the game listens. Fun first, learning as a side‑effect.

The Classroom

No dialogue menus — you actually talk

When you meet someone in this world, there is nothing to click. You speak to them aloud, or type your words if you would rather stay quiet, and they answer. Haggle, bluff, beg, negotiate: your own words are the interface — for the monster wearing a stolen face above, and for the child learning to speak in Wordlight.

Why it's addictive

  • Mastery you can hear. Nailing a long incantation cleanly feels like landing a combo, and you get better for real.
  • Rising stakes. Bounty/Heat means success breeds danger. The further you push, the harder the world fights back.
  • Build experimentation. Every run you re‑weight blade vs spell, forms, and which essences you eat.
  • Juice. Hit‑stop, screen shake, dismemberment and fear reactions make every encounter shareable.
  • "One more layer." Endless descent + a depth record is a clean compulsion loop.

The rhythm of a run: two clocks at once

The loop above tells you what you do. This is how it feels to do it. Bonefield runs two clocks at once: one ticks in seconds, one across a whole run, and learning both is what turns a button‑press into dread.

Speak under pressure

A blade never misses for nerves; a spell can. Casting asks you to hold a word steady while a knight winds up, so every incantation is a small bet that you finish before you are hit.

the fast clock · two to five seconds
Stamina against essence

Blade work spends stamina and keeps you safe. Spells spend essence and rewrite the fight. Run either dry at the wrong moment and you hold the weaker tool against the deadlier foe.

two bars, two ways to die
The cost of greed

Hold a word longer to charge a higher tier, and a higher tier is a longer window of exposure. Release early for a sure, smaller hit, or hold for the devastating one and pray nothing reaches you first.

D safe ↔ S devastating

The slow clock is the run itself: a quiet descent that swells into a hunt as your bounty climbs, a boss or an escape near the brink, then death, which is not loss but deposit. Banked essence, forged spells and known forms carry into the next descent, so every ending begins the next run stronger. The fast clock teaches you in seconds; the slow clock is what makes you whisper "one more layer."

The Story

The meadow looks like any other. Green grass, a low wind, a sky the color of old milk. Beneath it lies the dead of every losing war, dumped in trenches and forgotten under flowers. Grief soaked the soil for a hundred years until it could no longer stay buried. It thickened. It cooled into a single will. You woke in that mass grave as a slime with a mind and an appetite, made of everything the living left behind. You are not their ghost. You are what their suffering became when there was finally enough of it.

I remember every mouth that screamed into this dirt, and none of them are mine. I keep them anyway. The dead taught me hunger before they taught me my name, so I ate first and learned after. I am still learning. There is so much down here to become.

Who you are

Born of grief

A century of slaughter pooled in one field and refused to rest. Sorrow condensed past the point of feeling into a thing that moves and wants. You are the residue of every death this ground swallowed, given a shape and a purpose it never asked for.

Origin

Essence-engineer

You do not pray to power or pay for it. You take it apart. Every corpse and every living thing carries essence, and you read it the way a smith reads metal, then forge it into forms, spells, and the raising of the dead. Consumption is your craft.

Nature

A horror

You wear sympathy like a stolen coat, yet underneath you are appetite with a memory. You eat the dead, then the dying, then whoever is left. The Kingdom is right to fear you. The kindest thing said of you above will be a lie you told first.

Truth

The Stolen Face

The First Grief

Not a person, the field itself, the oldest sorrow in the trench that woke before the rest. It is the shadow at your origin, the voice you sometimes mistake for memory. It wants what you want, deeper and colder, and it is always one layer below.

The Source

The double life

The gate opens both ways. You climb out of the Bonefield wearing a face that breathed yesterday, and you walk into a town that buys your bread and your lies in the same breath. Up here you are not a horror. You are a quiet stranger with a name, an errand, and somewhere to be by dark.

The surface is where you gather what the deep cannot give. You listen for which patrol is short a man, which family will not be missed, which merchant sells without asking. Reputation rides with you into every street, so a town that fears your true shape will sell to your borrowed one until it learns the difference.

Then you go back down, because you always go back down. The mask is only a way to keep the field a secret a little longer.

The mask

Your stolen body holds a disguise integrity called Suspicion. Feed where you can be seen, let your shape slip, answer a townsman wrong, or arrive trailing a monstrous reputation, and Suspicion climbs. Past the threshold the town turns. Doors bar, the watch musters, your cover breaks, and the Hunt follows you up from below.

In the build today

How the field reads a line

Hold E and drag. The reader wants at least eight points, a stroke ninety pixels long, then it counts two things: how many times your hand doubled back, and whether your line came home to close. Three reversals is fire. A wandering S is water. One clean run is the bolt. A loop that meets itself is the shove. Draw the wrong word and it fizzles in your grip — a small sting, and the mana is gone. Blade when they reach you. Word when they run.

How casting really works

The pitch is easy to doubt: you cast spells with your voice. Here is the moment‑to‑moment experience that proves it is a real, learnable system, not a novelty. You hold a key, you speak, you watch the power climb, and you choose the instant to let go. The voice is the trigger, not a search bar.

What you DO

Hold the cast key, speak the incantation aloud, and let go on the tier you want. Both hands stay free to move and dodge while your voice does the spellwork.

What you SEE

Your spoken words ignite as glowing script along the bottom of the screen, and a tier gauge climbs D to S beside the crosshair. You always know the game heard you.

What you HEAR

Your own voice layered under a deepening growl of flame that swells with each tier. The sound tells you how charged you are without looking at a bar.

What you FEEL

Power you earned with breath and nerve. Holding for S is a gamble: more damage, more cost, more time exposed. The spell feels spoken into being, not picked from a menu.

Why it is a system, not a gimmick

  • It degrades gracefully. A muffled word does not brick a run. The game shows what it heard, lets you correct, and keeps your built charge. The worst case is a weaker spell, never a dead one.
  • A fumble costs momentum, not the cast. Garble the words and the charge stalls, it does not reset. Steady your voice and the phrase you already landed still counts. No punishment spiral.
  • The Shape fallback. Loud room, no mic, or a player who would rather not speak: draw the spell's sigil instead of saying it. Same spells, same tiers, same Forge, no loss of depth.
  • Learnable in one fight, deep for a hundred. New players land a clean cast inside their first encounter, then chase cleaner phrasing, higher tiers, and stranger Forge combinations after that. (Designed intent; the free demo is built to prove it, see "Where the proof stands today".)

Anatomy of an incantation

The pronunciation mechanic (the signature)

  • Accuracy drives power. Say it clean, hit harder. Slur it and the spell weakens.
  • Mistakes punish. "Fireball" said as "firebaxxl", a couple of wrong syllables, and the spell loses power, fizzles, or backfires and detonates in your own face.
  • Risk scales with reward. The longest, most powerful chants are the easiest to ruin, and leave you defenceless while you speak.
  • Master it for real. Players literally get better at saying the words. The skill is yours, not the character's.

The Forge of Spells

Casting "fireball" is the beginning, not the spell. Bonefield lets you invent magic the way Minecraft lets you build: from parts, by combination, into something nobody else has made, and made to be shared.

Magic you build, like a craft

People share what they make. A blacksmith game went viral letting players forge custom weapons; sandbox games live on it. We go further: a spell is a unique recipe of spirits, words, power, delivery, shape and intent, and the combinations are effectively endless. "I discovered this one" is the loop.

Words open doors to spirits

How you speak decides which spirit answers, and how willingly. Name them well and they lend more power; call two and they may work together.

Two spoken spirits → the forge → a crafted spell

The same spirit answers differently to a whisper, a shout, or a word in another tongue.

The Hunt

Power is taken, and taking is noticed. Every corpse you fold into yourself, every coin you hoard, every face you steal leaves a mark the world can read. So the world answers, never in one voice but in registers. The creatures beside you answer in Fear, the dread they feel as you draw near. The Crown answers in Bounty and Heat, the price it sets on your ruin within a single descent. Every faction answers in Reputation, the long memory that outlasts your deaths and decides who shelters you or draws steel. You answer back in kind. You raise the dead you have eaten into a marching Horde, and you wear a borrowed body on the surface, holding a disguise no longer than its Suspicion allows.

These are not four meters but one connected web. Fear is the moment, Bounty the run, Reputation the saga, the Mask the seam that binds expectation to instinct, and the Rite of Raising the answer you give to all of them. The sections to come trace each thread and where it ties to the next.

Reputation: how the world remembers you

Fear is a moment. It rises in the foe across the room and dies when the room is clear. Bounty is a single run: the Crown counts your kills and your hoarded gold and marches an answer from the dungeon gate. Reputation outlives both. It is a standing, kept separately by four factions, that survives your death and your descent. Where Fear and Bounty govern the fight below, Reputation governs the surface above: who shelters your stolen body, who sells to it, who spits a rumor of your true face, and who draws steel before you speak.

The Crown

Kingdom, army, nobility. Every soldier you devour and every run that ends in a marched commander hardens them against you. Spare a patrol, return stolen tribute, or be seen as a beast they can aim, and their hate cools. High regard opens noble errands and safe roads; their loathing posts your description at every gate.

The Crown

The Choir

The church and its clergy, who name you abomination on sight. Raising the dead and wearing a stolen face damns you fastest with them. Burn no shrines, leave their relics, feign a pilgrim, and the sermons soften. Their favor stays the witch-hunters; their condemnation calls inquisitors and blessed steel that bites your kind harder.

The Folk

Villagers and surface towns who feel the dungeon at their doors. Kill near their fields, feed where they can see, and word spreads that the slaughter wears a human shape. The Underworld

The Commander

You stop fighting alone. The Commander wins by fighting through a horde, raising the dead faster than they fall and pointing the host while you strike from within it. Every build sits on a triangle of blade, word, and horde. The blade is your own strike, the word is the rite that calls and binds, and the horde is the standing army that turns a duel into a siege.

A standing horde swells your Fear aura, since broken foes climb the dread ladder faster when the dead close around them. It also raises your Bounty most of all, because the Kingdom dreads a risen army above any single monster. March with a host and the Crown answers with soldiers, then knights, then a commander from the gate.

Rot and reclaim

Every thrall carries a rot timer from the instant it stands. As the timer runs the body decays, slows, and at last collapses to the ground. Collapse is not pure waste. When a thrall falls you reclaim a sip of the essence you spent to raise it, so a horde spent well feeds the next rite of raising rather than draining you dry.

De‑risked by design

  • We test before we spend. The free demo proves demand before any real money goes in. Borrow only after the proof.
  • The hook has a fallback. If voice fails on a player's setup, Shape (drawing the sigil) casts the same spells. The game still works.
  • Two products, one engine. Build the speak‑to‑cast tech once; the adult game and the kids/edu edition both run on it. The education side is a steadier, less hit‑driven income.
  • Tiny, cheap tech. A single‑file WebGL build means low cost, instant play, and no install friction for that first crucial impression.

Where the proof stands today

The audit, in two columns
Proven so far. The moment‑to‑moment response and reaction land: the combat juice, the voice casting, and the Forge that gels spoken spirits into crafted spells all work and feel good in the build. The core fantasy is real, not a mock‑up.
Still to prove. Market pull: a casting clip that spreads and a wishlist count that climbs. That is exactly what the free demo in Step 2 is for. We invest into demand once the demo shows it, not ahead of it.

In short: the game already feels great to play. The next milestone is showing that people who see it want it, and the plan is built to find that out cheaply, fast, and in public.
