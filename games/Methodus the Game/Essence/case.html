Chapter VI

The Horizon

What comes after the demo is not a promise, it is a road.

Each step is small on purpose. Each one earns the right to the next, and none of them lie about what is already in your hands. The field you play today is the first light on a path that bends toward the far shore, where the Word, the Sign, and the Blade all live in one body. Below is the whole of it, honest about what is lit and what is only imagined.

Five waypoints on the descent that rises. The first is lit and playable today, the field and the voice both; the rest are the design, not the deed. No dates, only order, and only the ones we mean to keep.

  1. Waypoint I · Now · Live

    Essence Alpha and the voice prototype

    The one waypoint that glows as done. Everything it names is already in your hands.

  2. Waypoint II · Next

  3. Waypoint III · Then

  4. Waypoint IV · Then

  5. Waypoint V · Beyond

What is lit, and what is only drawn

Play the alpha and the voice today. Everything past Steam is honest intention, not a shipped feature. When a waypoint lights, this page will say so in the same breath it says everything else. Two schools are yours already: blade when they reach you, word when they run. The rest is the road.

02What the road carries

The Sign

Sign in the air

Shape the sigil with your hand and the webcam reads it: no mouse, no mic, and both hands stay in the fight. In the build today the camera mode is a stub that tells you the truth: “camera gesture coming soon, draw instead.”

The Silence

Cast without a sound

A loud room, a sleeping house, a player who would rather not speak. Silent casting keeps the door open when the voice cannot, and play that leans on the hands welcomes signers: the third school is drawn with them in mind.

The Packs

Every tongue a grimoire

The field listens in English (en-US) only today. Each language pack teaches it a new tongue, so a caster can chant in the words they were raised on: recognition opens per language, one grimoire at a time.

The Forbidden

The forbidden lines

Some spells only awaken in their own language: a forbidden line spoken in Japanese, a war-word in another tongue. They never answer to translation, and the one who learns to say them owns them. Say them wrong and they may turn on you.

The Classroom

WORDLIGHT

The same engine that hears your incantation can hear a child’s first clean sentence. The classroom edition rides this exact recognition: your pronunciation is your power, in the field and in the lesson.

Platforms Beyond

Mobile, then console

Voice carries anywhere, so the phone is a natural home once touch controls are redesigned around a thumb, not a keyboard. Console waits until the web and Steam prove the hook: the controller waits until success earns it. Planned, in order, none of them today.

The Ages Beyond

One law, many ages

Essence science is written to survive the jump: a wand today, a warship's reactor in some far age, the same law of vessels and projection. Bonefield is the first chapter of that physics.

Each step small, each one earning the next: the field you play today is only the first light on the road.

Chapter VII

Launch & The Case

Five milestones to launch, two markets from one engine, and the honest, audited case for why it wins.

Open the one-page investor brief

The brief above is a clean, sendable summary of everything below. The long version starts here. Ship fast, but ship well, with a hook that holds. Voice casting, the game's loudest gift, is the marketing, and it is free; the road to paid revenue is the one with the least risk.

The path is web demo first, then Steam paid launch. The game is already a single-file WebGL build, so a free, instant-play demo on itch.io and the web costs almost nothing and reaches players today. Use it to prove the hook, gather clips, and build a Steam wishlist. Then ship the full, polished game on Steam, where PC players actually pay.

01Who buys it

The Core

Roguelite and action fans

The Hades, Vampire Survivors and Risk of Rain crowd. They buy day one and stay for the descent.

The Reach

Streamers and clip viewers

They want chaotic on-camera moments, and they spread the game free. The voice hook is what crosses from core into reach.

The Long Tail

Language learners and classrooms

Drawn by the pronunciation hook. Each ring out is bigger and cheaper to reach.

02The plan, in plain words

Five steps, in order, all inside twelve months. Each one is small, and each one earns the right to the next.

  1. Months 0 to 1

    Build one beautiful slice

    One gorgeous layer with the full loop: casting, melee, dismemberment, fear, a few spells. This is the 60 seconds that sells everything.

  2. Months 1 to 2

    Put a free demo online

    Anyone can play it in a browser. Cut a short trailer led by a voice-cast moment. Open a Steam page and start collecting wishlists.

  3. Months 2 to 5

    Add content, grow a crowd

    More layers, spells and enemies. Post clips every day. Start a Discord and run pronunciation challenges so players bring players.

  4. Months 5 to 8

    Sell it on Steam, Early Access

    The first real money. Price it low, improve it with player data, and ride the roguelite "just one more run" pull.

  5. Months 8 to 12

    Full launch, then keep feeding it

    The 1.0 release with a soundtrack and optional cosmetics, then regular content drops (new depths) to keep players coming back.

04How we pay for it

Climb a ladder. Spend almost nothing to prove the hook, and let proof unlock the next rung. Only take on money once people have shown they want it. Debt before proof is the most common indie mistake.

  1. Step 0 · now

    Bootstrap

    Build the slice with what we already have. Cost: time plus near-free tools. Risk: none. Nobody funds a game they cannot play, so this comes first.

  2. Step 1 · free

    Prove it

    Ship the web demo and post clips. The single question that unlocks money: do casting clips get shared, and do wishlists climb? If yes, run a Kickstarter; proof is exactly what makes crowdfunding work.

  3. Step 2 · preferred

    Kickstarter

    Launch with a polished demo, a trailer, and a following. It funds 6 to 12 months of focused work, and the backers become the launch-day audience and reviewers. The best trade of risk and ownership.

  4. Step 3 · onward

    Self-sustaining

    Early Access and Steam sales repay any debt and fund the 1.0. From here the game pays for itself.

The Preferred Route

Kickstarter

No debt, you keep ownership, and backers become a built-in launch audience; it proves demand before you over-invest. The price: it needs a polished demo, a trailer and a following first, a miss is public, and fulfilment takes real time.

The Last Resort

A small family loan

Fast, flexible, no platform cut, patient terms over one to two years, no public risk. The price: personal relationships at stake, real debt with real repayment, and no marketing benefit, just cash. Written down, small, never plan A.

If we raise, where it goes. Lean by design: most of any raise buys focused dev time, not overhead.

06Two editions, two markets

Why education is a real second market

One voice-casting engine, two products. The dark roguelite is the flagship for adults; a bright, gentle sibling carries the same speak-to-cast magic into homes and classrooms.

Speaking to cast is, by happy accident, a pronunciation drill kids enjoy. That makes Wordlight fundable in ways games rarely are: parent subscriptions, school and district licences, the ESL and language-learning markets, and education grants. The adult game proves the tech and pays the bills; the kid edition opens a durable, less hit-driven revenue stream. Build the engine once, dress it twice.

07Why it wins: the audit

Not a wish, an audit. Here is the honest case for why this game can succeed, what already proves it, and where the proof still has to come from.

What we already know is public record: combination out-scales hand-built content; it is the engine under the biggest hits of the decade. People pay full price for a roguelite that feels great, and a tiny team can reach millions: Hades did, Vampire Survivors did. Browser voice recognition is now fast enough to drive an action game with no install. What we must still prove is ours to prove: that spoken combination feels as good as it reads on paper, that our moment-to-moment combat clears the quality bar, that the mic holds up in a loud living room, and that our specific clips travel, measured in wishlists.

Look closely at the biggest, longest-living hits of the last decade and one shape repeats. Hand players simple parts, let them combine those parts into outcomes the developers never authored, and make the results easy to show off. That is the engine under Minecraft. Bonefield runs the same engine, aimed at the action-roguelite that has never had it.

Bonefield's wager is to bring that system to spell-casting, spoken aloud, where no breakout has gone yet.

Read down a column and you find a famous game that already proved players love that feature, so the demand is real. Read across the Bonefield row and it is the only game with a mark in every column. That is the pitch: not one trick, the whole set.

The pattern is not that rivals are weak; each is excellent at its slice. Bonefield's edge is the one sentence that ties the slices together: you speak a spell aloud, invent it by combining spirits in the Forge, and the result is yours to share.

The Wedge

Voice as the cast

No combat roguelite casts by spoken word. Skyrim and Baldur's Gate only flavour it; Bonefield makes the voice the input, a ground-up model a rival cannot bolt on in a patch. And if a mic fails, Shape casting draws the same spells, so the game never breaks.

The Forge

Invent, then run

Noita and Magicka combine spells, but neither marries it to a premium roguelite loop built to be shared. You discover a spell, name it, and share it like a Minecraft build. The grimoire is endless, and it is yours.

The Fuel

Streamer fuel by design

Speaking spells on camera is funny and clip-worthy before any marketing spend. Backfires and two-minute apocalypses film themselves. The hook is the ad.

Chapter VIII

The Studio

A solo developer building Bonefield in public: who is behind it, what the build needs, and how to reach him.

Bazil Zieel is the founder and, for now, the whole studio. Solo designer and developer: he directs the vision and builds the whole thing, the voice casting, the Forge, the combat, and the long descent.

Built solo on purpose, but open to the right collaborators (art, audio) and backers. If you want to help build it or cover it, the door is open.

01How it is built

Renderer

Three.js r160

Full 3D scene: character, environment, lighting, and VFX on a live GPU canvas.

Voice casting

Web Speech API

Native browser voice recognition. No server, no third party. Real-time and offline-capable.

Game logic

Vanilla JS

No framework overhead. Game loop, forge system, and UI on a custom engine, no bundler.

Audio: planned

Howler.js

Planned: spatial audio for spells, hit reactions, and the ambient sounds of the descent.
