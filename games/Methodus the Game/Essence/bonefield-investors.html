The Wedge

A category nobody owns

Growth

Three loops that compound

Most games have one growth loop. Essence has three, and each one feeds the next.

Virality

Clip it

Play → clip a moment → others watch → wishlist → play

Backfires and 2-minute apocalypses film themselves. The hook is inherently shareable.

Creativity

Forge it

Craft a spell → discover something → share the recipe → others try it

Content the players make for free, like Minecraft builds. A low-content retention engine.

Mastery

Master it

Speak → get it cleaner → hit harder → feel yourself improve

The skill is real and yours, so it keeps pulling you back. Pronunciation is the progression.

Editions

Two markets, one engine

Build the speak-to-cast tech once; dress it twice. Revenue is not a single bet.

Adult flagship

Essence

Dark action-roguelite: gore, fear, the long descent. Premium ~$15–19 on Steam + web. The brand and the main revenue.

Kids / edu

Wordlight

Bright, gentle, zero gore. A young apprentice learns real words by speaking spells. Freemium + school/district licenses + parent subscription, a steadier, less hit-driven stream.

Roadmap

The plan: 12 months, five milestones

Each step is small, and each one earns the right to the next.

  1. Step 1 · 0–1 mo

    Build one beautiful slice

    One gorgeous layer with the full loop: casting, melee, dismemberment, fear, a few spells. The 60 seconds that sells everything.

  2. Step 2 · 1–2 mo

    Put a free demo online

    Instant-play in a browser. Cut a voice-led trailer, open a Steam page, start collecting wishlists.

  3. Step 3 · 2–5 mo

    Add content, grow a crowd

    More layers, spells, enemies. Daily clips, a Discord, pronunciation challenges so players bring players.

  4. Step 4 · 5–8 mo

    Sell on Steam (Early Access)

    The first real money. Price low, improve with player data, ride the “just one more run” pull.

  5. Step 5 · 8–12 mo

    Full 1.0, then keep feeding it

    Soundtrack, optional cosmetics, regular content drops (new depths) to keep players coming back.

The funding ladder

Only take on money once people have shown they want it. Debt before proof is the most common indie mistake.

  1. Step 0 · Bootstrap

    Build the slice

    With what we already have. Cost: time plus near-free tools. Risk: none. Nobody funds a game they cannot play.

  2. Step 1 · Prove it

    Ship the free demo

    The one question that unlocks money: do casting clips get shared, and do wishlists climb?

  3. Step 2 · Preferred

    Kickstarter

    Launch with a polished demo, a trailer, and a following. Funds 6–12 months; backers become your launch-day audience. Best trade of risk and ownership.

  4. Step 3 · Self-sustaining

    Early Access → 1.0

    Steam sales repay any debt and fund the full launch. From here the game pays for itself.

Risk

De-risked by design

  • We test before we spend. The free demo proves demand before any real money goes in. Borrow only after the proof.
  • The hook has a fallback. If voice fails on a player's setup, Shape (drawing the sigil) casts the same spells. The game still works.
  • Two products, one engine. Build the speak-to-cast tech once; the adult game and the kids/edu edition both run on it.
  • Tiny, cheap tech. A single-file WebGL build means low cost, instant play, and no install friction for that first impression.

Where the proof stands today

✓ Proven so far

The moment-to-moment response and reaction land: the combat juice, the voice casting, and the Forge that gels spoken spirits into crafted spells all work and feel good in the build. The core fantasy is real, not a mock-up.

→ Still to prove

Market pull: a casting clip that spreads and a wishlist count that climbs. That is exactly what the free demo is for. We invest into demand once the demo shows it, not ahead of it.

The game already feels great to play. The next milestone is showing that people who see it want it, and the plan is built to find that out cheaply, fast, and in public.
