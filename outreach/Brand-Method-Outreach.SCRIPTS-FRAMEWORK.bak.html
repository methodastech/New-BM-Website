How to use this.

Selling is a good conversation with a shape. These are the lines that hold the shape up. Read them once and every script below makes sense.

Say it aloud

If it does not sound like a person, rewrite it. Short lines. One idea at a time.

Lead value

Open with a signal or an observation, never a pitch. Give something before you ask for the call.

Listen 70/30

Ask a question, then go quiet and let them finish. The one who listens controls the conversation.

One next step

Every message ends with a single, easy thing to do. Never two asks in one line.

The warmth spectrum.

How well this person already knows us decides how you open and how hard you push. Work warm and hot first. They convert far faster than cold.

Cold

Never heard of us. Lead with a signal and a free teardown.

Warm

Knows us or was referred. Name the link and move to a call.

Hot

Asked about price or timeline. Move to close. Do not re-sell.

Sort your list warm to cold before you send a single message.

Stage 01 · Before the call

Cold outreach.

They do not know us yet. One specific observation beats a generic pitch every time. Offer value first, a free teardown, not a meeting.

Cold DM · LinkedIn or Instagram

Hi [name], saw [specific thing: your launch, funding, new product]. Congrats.

We build websites and apps that turn visitors into booked enquiries, live in weeks not months.

I pulled up [their site] and spotted one thing quietly costing you leads. Want a 90-second teardown, free? No pitch.

Why: a real observation earns the reply. You lead with a gift, so there is nothing to say no to yet.
Cold email · subject "one thing on [their site]"

Hi [name], quick one.

You are paying for traffic to [their site], but the first screen does not tell a visitor what to do next, so most of them leave.

We fix exactly that: sites and apps built to convert, fixed price, you own everything. Recent work is here: [Brand-Method-Sales-Page.html]

Want me to send a short teardown of where yours is leaking?

Why: names the leak and the cost, points to proof, ends with one easy yes.

Stage 01 · Before the call

Cold call & voicemail.

The goal of the call is not to sell. It is to earn twenty minutes later. Get in, be human, get out.

Cold call · opener

Hi [name], this is [you] from Brand Method. Quick one, is now a bad time?

I will be fast. I looked at your site and found where it is quietly losing enquiries. I help brands like yours fix that with a site or app built to sell.

Worth a 20-minute call this week where I show you exactly where the leak is? You keep the plan either way.

Why: "is now a bad time" earns permission. The offer is a diagnosis, not a pitch.
Voicemail · 15 seconds

Hi [name], [you] from Brand Method. I looked at your site and found where it is losing enquiries. I will send the detail by WhatsApp, have a look and tell me if I am wrong. Talk soon.

Why: leaves a reason to expect your message. The WhatsApp follow is where the real conversation happens.

Stage 01 · Before the call

Warm outreach.

They were referred, or they already engage with us. Name the link and go straight to the call. Do not over-explain what they already trust.

Referral

Hi [name], [referrer] said you are looking to [rebuild the site / launch the app].

That is exactly what we do: websites, apps and interactive builds made to sell, fixed price, you own all of it.

Free 20-minute call this week to map where your enquiries leak? [day] or [day]?

Why: the referrer's name does the trust work. You go straight to the calendar.

Stage 01 · Before the call

They enquired.

They came to us. Speed wins. Reply fast, keep it short, and put the next step in their hands.

Inbound reply

Thanks for reaching out, [name].

Quickest way to help is a short call where I map exactly where your site or app is leaking and put a number on what it costs you.

Does [day/time] or [day/time] work? Takes 20 minutes, and you keep the plan whether you build with us or not.

Why: two time options is easier to answer than "when are you free". No risk framing lowers the guard.

Stage 01 · Before the call

They went quiet.

Silence is not a no. Follow up with a rhythm and a reason, never "just checking in". Add value each time, then close the loop cleanly.

Day 2

Did the teardown make sense? Happy to walk you through it live in 15 minutes.

Day 5 · proof

Here is a site we built for [client], live in [X] days. Same approach I would use for you: [link].

Day 9 · close

I will stop here so I am not chasing you. If the timing is not now, when should I check back?

Why: the clean close often gets the reply the nudges did not. It respects their time and reopens the door.

Stage 02 · On the call

Discovery.

Do not skip this. It is where most beginners lose the sale. Ask, then listen. You are finding the leak in their words, so the fix sounds like their idea.

  • When someone lands on your site for the first time, what do you want them to do?
  • How many of them actually do it right now?
  • Where do most of your deals start today, the site, a referral, or ads?
  • If we fixed one thing in the next 30 days, what would move the needle most?
  • What have you tried before, and what did it cost you?
Why: these five surface the leak, the budget and the urgency without a single pitch. Write down their exact phrases and use them at the close.

Stage 02 · On the call

Recommend & quote.

Match the build to what they just told you. One clear recommendation, tied to their words, at a fixed price. Do not present a menu, present a path.

Website

They pay for traffic and lose it. Turn visitors into booked enquiries. Starter from RM1,900, Growth from RM6,000.

App

They need something customers open daily. A product, prototype-approved before any code.

Game / interactive

They need attention and a hook. An experience people play with and remember.

The recommendation

Based on what you said about [their exact words], I would start with a [website / app], because [reason tied to their words].

Fixed price, live in [timeline], and you own the code, the content and the data. No lock-in.

Why: a fixed price and ownership remove the two biggest fears. Tying it to their words makes it feel built for them.

Stage 02 · On the call

Objections.

A pushback is not a no. It is a worry in disguise. Answer the worry, then ask one question back to keep the conversation moving.

"Too expensive"

Fair. The real question is the return, not the price. One extra enquiry a week covers this. Let me show you where those enquiries are leaking now. How many do you think you are losing a month?

Worry: will it pay back. Move: reframe to return, then quantify the leak.
"We are planning to update it anyway"

Then you are exactly the right person to see this. Most updates change the paint. We rebuild the part that turns visitors into enquiries. Want me to show you the difference on your own page?

Worry: we can do it ourselves. Move: separate decoration from conversion.
"Send me something first"

That is the call. It is faster to show you live than to type it, and you will get the plan to keep either way. [day] or [day]?

Worry: another sales trap. Move: the call is the value, and it is free.
"I need to think about it"

Of course. So I answer the right thing, what is the one part you need to be sure of before you decide?

Worry: a hidden concern. Move: surface it instead of chasing air.

Stage 02 · On the call

Close the deal.

Make one clear offer and one easy next step. Then go quiet. The first person to talk after the ask loses.

The close

Here is what I would do.

We build [offering] as one system, fixed price, live in [timeline]. You own all of it.

Step one is a short working session where we map the leak and put a number on it. You keep the plan whether you build with us or not.

Shall I send the times?

Why: low-risk first step, clear ownership, one question. Then stop talking and let them answer.

Stage 03 · After the yes

Kickoff.

The yes is fragile until the deposit lands and work starts. Move fast, look organised, and make them feel senior hands are already on it.

Confirm & start

Great, let us lock it in. I will send the [deposit link / invoice] and a short intake so we start on the right foot.

You will hear from a senior person, not a junior, within 24 hours. Work starts [date].

Why: speed and a named next step protect the sale. "Senior, not junior" is the promise that closed them, kept on day one.

Stage 03 · After the yes

Delivery & handover.

Hand over more than a file. Hand over control, proof it works, and make them look good to their own team.

Handover

Here is your build, live. The code, the content and the data are yours.

Two things before we wrap: I will set up [analytics access], and book a 15-minute walkthrough so your team can run it without us.

Why: ownership and a walkthrough turn a delivery into trust. Trust is what earns the referral.

Stage 03 · After the yes

Ask for the referral.

The best time to ask is right after a win. Make it specific and easy, one name, one intro.

The ask

One favour. If this made your life easier, who is one other founder leaking enquiries the same way you were?

A quick intro is the best thanks you can give, and I will look after them the same way.

Why: "one name" is easy to answer. Asked warm, right after value, it is the cheapest pipeline you will ever build.
