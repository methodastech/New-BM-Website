Web Plan · Brand Method

How the new site is built to work

One Canva page becomes a fourteen page site with a spine. This document is the plan the prototype was built from and the plan the full build will be held to: what was decided, the argument, every page, every audience, how the pages link, how it looks, and what happens after launch. Anything in the prototype that contradicts this document is the prototype's error.

The plan in five lines

One page becomes a site with a spine. Three service pages, a coverage set, proof, pricing, FAQ, About, the Adinita programme and a real footer. Each one is a door from search that the current single page cannot be.

One booking path replaces two systems and a dead end. AbiliCar, Lady Driver and Chaperone book through one flow that captures a phone number and hands off to WhatsApp.

The credentials get sentences, not logos. PERKESO, JKM, Yayasan Hasanah, UKM, UPM, UTM, UiTM and the hospitals each get one line explaining the relationship.

Accessibility is the build standard, not a feature. 16 px body copy, 44 px targets, real headings, written alt text, keyboard operable, AA contrast.

Bahasa Melayu ships with English, not after it. The buyer is often the adult child, but the passenger and most Adinita drivers read BM first.

The strategy, and how the site is organised to carry it

A position that never reaches a page is a document, not a decision
What this section is

The position Jom Makcik takes, and the exact places on the site where that position becomes visible.

Why it matters

Every operator in the competitor set has a position and none of them show it, which is why they all read the same. The current jommakcik.my is the same: seven years of institutional trust presented as an unlabelled logo wall.

What we want from it

Agreement on the argument before Friday, so every later section can be checked against it rather than debated again.

The brand decisionHow the site expresses it, and where
Positioning

The only operator in Malaysia that both carries a passenger seated in their own wheelchair and sends a trained woman who walks them in. Not a taxi with a bigger boot.

The hero says it in six words, Stay in your own wheelchair. We walk you in. The comparison block then proves it against the largest competitor's own published limits. Nothing on the page asks the visitor to infer the difference.

The reason to believe

Founded by a rehabilitation medicine lecturer whose own patients were missing therapy for want of a ride.

The founder section: her quote, her signature, her credential. Today her clinical background is not on the site at all. It is the strongest trust signal in medical transport and it opens the About page.

The proof

PERKESO, JKM, Yayasan Hasanah, AirAsia Foundation, UKM, UPM, UTM, UiTM, two specialist hospitals, roughly 600 trained drivers.

Three placements. A colour marquee directly under the hero for recognition. A flat card rail on Home with one sentence per partner for meaning. A full Partners page for the referrer who needs to check.

The market fact

68% of Malaysian seniors avoid medical appointments because they cannot get there. Published by Yayasan Hasanah, one of their own partners, three weeks ago.

The first number in the stat band, and the opening line of the Dialysis and Hospital pages. The problem statement is theirs to own because their partner published it.

The commercial wedge

Nobody commercial in this category publishes a price. Only the NGO does.

A Pricing section on Home and a Pricing page, with written quotes promised before booking. Rates are shown as On request until Dr Sazlina confirms them; the structure is already built to hold real numbers.

The workforce

Adinita: adiwira wanita, female heroes. Roughly 600 women on a 75% revenue share.

Today a login button. On the new site a named programme page, Drive with us, that recruits as well as reassures, and a figure in the stat band on Home.

The language

The buyer is often the adult child reading English; the passenger and most drivers read Malay first.

Every string on every page in both languages, switched by the flag toggle in the header, remembered between visits, with the document language set correctly for screen readers.

The five linking rules
Rule 01Every service page links to Pricing and Coverage

The two questions a family asks after "can you take her" are "what does it cost" and "do you come to us". Both answered one click away, always.

Rule 02Every page ends at Book

One ask per page, never two competing buttons. Book is the last thing on every page and the sticky action on every phone.

Rule 03Proof sits beside the ask, not in a separate section

Drivers and existing clients know where to look. New customers never see a login before a booking button.

How the site looks and feels

The design system as built in Prototype Web 1, and the rules it obeys
What this section is

The design system settled and already live in the prototype: grid, spacing, components, colour, type, imagery and motion.

Why it matters

Fourteen pages built by different hands only look like one site if the system is agreed first. It is also where the premium bar is met or lost.

What we want from it

Sign off on the direction, and a date for the photo shoot, because the generated portraits and scenes are holding measures.

The rules, from the review
GroundWhite throughout

Ink #0E1424 for text. No cream, no grey ground, no warm tint anywhere. Cards and photographs separate from the page with a soft shadow and one 4px radius. Dark is the banner film and nothing else.

LinesNone

No rules between stats, no grid borders, no dividers. Whitespace, ground colour, and soft shadow separate things.

EyebrowsNone

Sections open on the heading. No uppercase kicker above it.

TypePoppins and Plus Jakarta Sans

Poppins 600 for display, 500 for headings, because it matches the wordmark. Plus Jakarta Sans for all body and small text. No monospace on the client site.

ColourOne red, disciplined

#F91B2A for the mark, figures and emphasis. #C60815 for buttons, so white text passes AA. #FFF3F4 as the only wash.

PunctuationNo dashes as separators

Colons, commas and full stops. Applies in Malay too.

BoxesOnly where a card carries an image

Service cards and the comparison are cards with shadow. FAQ, team and partner entries sit flat on the ground.

TitlesOne line when the words fit

No width cap on headings. A short title sits on one line.

Imagery

Every image on the prototype is theirs or made for them: the logo cropped into a horizontal lockup, eight service photographs, four portraits, fourteen partner logos, and two generated documentary scenes for the comparison. Nothing is stock.

The open asset request. The studio portraits are warm brown on a cream backdrop and read as a family studio, not a mobility service. The prototype shows them in the khaki field vest, generated from those photographs, as a holding measure. The brand needs a half day shoot: the team in the khaki field vest on a neutral ground beside a vehicle, the ramp in use with a real passenger, a chaperone walking someone in, and Dr Sazlina's real signature scanned.
Motion
  • The banner film is atmosphere, not content. The copy never depends on it.
  • The marquee and the partner rail move at walking pace and pause on hover.
  • Cards lift 5 px on hover with the image scaling 1.05. Nothing else moves.
  • The play button carries a slow pulse ring, the one piece of decoration on the page.
  • The Brand Method ribbon waves as cloth, gated by an IntersectionObserver.
  • Every one of these stops under prefers-reduced-motion, and the rails become scrollable so nothing is clipped.

What happens after launch

Templates that carry growth, handover, and the care model
What this section is

What happens once the site is live: the three templates that carry growth, handover, training and the care model.

Why it matters

A site that cannot be extended without a rebuild starts decaying on launch day. Three templates mean a new area, a new service or a new partner is an entry, not a project.

What we want from it

Clarity on who owns what after handover, since prices, hours and coverage need a named owner in the office or they go stale the way the current site did.

At handover, what the office gets on day one
H1
A live walkthrough, recorded

One session with whoever runs the office. Changing a price, adding a partner, updating hours, done live. The recording stays with Jom Makcik for the next person.

H2
Short guides, one job each

Change a rate. Add or remove an area. Add a testimonial with a name. Swap a photograph. Update the hours everywhere at once.

H3
Keys in Jom Makcik's name

Domain, hosting, analytics and the Google Business Profile stay in the company's accounts. We work inside them, we do not hold them.

The audit rerun, in public

The same rig, run again after launch, with before and after published. It either improved or it did not, and the numbers say which.

H5
React ready handoff

Built to the house handoff standard so an in house or agency developer can take it into React or Next.js without a rewrite.

H6
Care, not silence

A named person to call when something breaks, and a check on the numbers each month rather than an invoice and a disappearance.
