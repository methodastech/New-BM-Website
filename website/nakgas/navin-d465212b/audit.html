What changed on 1 Oct

The model is settled

No riders. The dealers who already deliver take the order. Suppliers pay nothing. The customer pays one fee per order. Three of the four money questions of 29 Sep now have an answer; the fee figure does not.

What holds the launch

Money and paperwork, not code

The first round is not in, the company that signs is not confirmed, and Apple needs 15 to 20 days once it is. None of these is a build task.

The same dealer, in the phone

Every app rival we found in the Peninsula runs its own or freelance riders, or does not say where it delivers. None states Johor coverage (read 6 Oct). The opening is real, and one rival with the dealer model is already live as a web app.

The model

The dealers who already deliver, no riders of our own, and an order engine that always ends.

Fact · Navin, 1 Oct

No riders

Rivals run their own drivers or "agents" in the Klang Valley. NakGas keeps none: the dealer who delivers by phone today takes the order and delivers it himself.

Fact · Navin, 1 Oct

Dealers pay nothing

No commission and no fee to the supplier. It is the bait that gets them on board.

Fact · call, 28 Sep

The customer never chooses

The platform assigns the nearest available licensed shop or truck. Not a marketplace.

One order through the engine

  1. Step 1Customer ordersSaved address flagged landed or high-rise, size, quantity, new or swap, now or scheduled.
  2. Step 2Nearest dealerThe nearest available shop or truck within its radius and with stock.
  3. Step 345 secondsThe dealer sees a countdown and accepts or declines.
  4. Step 4Next nearestOn a decline or a timeout the order moves on.
  5. Step 5Admin after fourFour declines in a row and the order escalates to Admin.

Sources: call of 28 Sep, 00:58:31 to 01:07:54; his developer's scope v1.1, CR-09 and features 19 to 23. The scope wrote 45 to 60 seconds; we fix 45 seconds, editable in Admin settings.

Radius. On 28 Sep Navin put the viable trip at about two kilometres for a small delivery charge (01:01:33). One live rival rejects addresses more than 2.5 km from its dealer (GasXpress site code, read 6 Oct). Tracking. Status pings with the driver's confirmation, not a live map, in Phase One.
Our read. The engine is the product and it is not the risk. The risk is whether drivers who work for distributors keep the supplier app open. With no riders of our own there is no fallback fleet: if the nearest truck is offline, the nearest available may be far away.

Delivery is charged per cylinder, as in the brief's two cylinder example (RM6.80). The gateway percentage is Navin's highest channel figure, so these are worst cases.

Our read, three things the arithmetic shows.
  1. The fee is per order but the gateway is a percentage of the whole basket, so a bigger order keeps less: about RM1.63 at three cylinders under option B.
  2. On cash on delivery the customer hands the dealer RM33.50, platform fee included. Suppliers pay nothing, yet the platform still has to collect its RM3.50 from the dealer, by netting it off his online payouts or by invoice. The settlement rule has to be written before the wallet is built (F29).
  3. At about RM2.83 kept, every 1,000 orders a month is about RM2,830 a month before servers, SMS, support and promotion. No demand figure exists yet, so this is arithmetic, not a forecast (F28).
Fact, read 6 Oct. Rivals charge the supply side: Gasbee's merchant page says dealers "only share a small commission on completed orders"; GasXpress's site code carries a configurable platform commission on the dealer. Navin's decks still describe a commission too (supplier deck p.11, admin deck p.162): they need updating to "suppliers pay nothing".
