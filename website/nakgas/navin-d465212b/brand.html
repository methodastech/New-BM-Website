      The problem: ordering gas is still a phone call

      LPG is a controlled item in Malaysia and the price of the gas is fixed by law. What is not fixed is everything around it: finding someone, reaching them, and knowing what you paid for.

      What happens todayWhat it costs the householdSource
      You find a number: a sticker on the cylinder, a neighbour, a search onlineTime, and no way to tell who is licensedFact Navin, call of 28 Sep 2026
      You call and wait. If the dealer does not pick up, you call the next oneThe cooking stops while you waitFact Navin, 28 Sep
      The price is what you are told on the phoneNo proof that RM26.60 is the gas and the rest is deliveryFact KPDN controlled price RM26.60 for 14 kg (RM1.90 a kg), set 8 Apr 2026. A Johor Bahru dealer charges RM30.00 all in by phone (Navin, 1 Oct)
      The tank runs out mid-cook. Only experience warns youA meal, and a rushed callFact Navin, call of 1 Oct 2026
      The apps that exist run their own riders or agents, mostly in the Klang ValleyOutside the central region the choice is mostly the phoneFact Navin, 1 Oct; audit chapter 2. Our read on the second column
      Our read. The real rival is not another app. It is the dealer’s phone number on a sticker. NakGas wins the day ordering is easier than calling, at a price the customer can check.

      The insight: the flame already tells you

      Three things we heard on the calls, and what we read into them.

      The blue flame

      A clean LPG flame burns blue. Navin, 1 Oct: “not all of us are like our mothers who know, the colour of the gas fire.” Our mothers read the flame to know the tank is going. Most of us cannot.

      NakGas does that reading for you: first a reminder from your own order rhythm, later (Phase Two) a smart regulator that knows the balance before the flame changes.

      Since 20 Oct the mark is the first flame in green, emerald with a mint inner cone, and the blue burn stays as a variation set, so the idea stays in the files.

      Fact: the flame, his wordsOur read: the role

      The phone call habit

      Today the order is a phone call: find the number, ring, wait, hope the price is right (Navin, 28 Sep).

      The habit to replace is the call, not the dealer. That is where the tagline comes from: Gas sampai. Tak payah call.

      Fact: how it works todayOur read: what to replace

      The dealers already on the road

      Licensed dealers already deliver at one price. Johor Bahru distributors refused to pay any commission, so suppliers pay nothing: it is what brings them on board (Navin, 1 Oct).

      The supply is there. What is missing is the order book. NakGas is that order book, free for the dealer.

      Fact: no commissionOur read: the order book

      The insight in one line

      People do not want a new gas company. They want their usual gas, without having to ask for it.

      Orang tak perlukan syarikat gas baharu. Mereka mahukan gas yang biasa, tanpa perlu menelefon sesiapa.

      Our read

      Positioning

      One line, in both languages, then the frame it is built on.

      The gas dealer you already trust, now in your phone: the controlled price first, one small fee, and no more calling around.

      Pengedar gas yang anda percaya, kini dalam telefon anda: harga kawalan dahulu, satu fi kecil, dan tak perlu lagi telefon sana sini.

      FrameNakGas
      ForHouseholds in Malaysia that cook with LPG, starting in Johor Bahru (launch region: our read of Navin’s base, to confirm)
      WhoRun out without warning and still order by phone
      NakGas isThe ordering app for the licensed dealers already on the road
      ThatShows the KPDN price first, adds one fee per order, and sends the order to the nearest dealer with stock, who has 45 seconds to accept
      UnlikeThe central-region apps that run their own riders or agents
      BecauseThe dealers deliver already, pay no commission, and every one has a KPDN licence on file with its expiry tracked

      What NakGas is not

      • Not a gas company. It sells no gas of its own.
      • Not a rider fleet. The dealer delivers, as today.
      • Not a discount app. “Cheapest” is never the claim: the gas price is fixed by law.
      • Not a cartoon. The market already has a yellow cartoon bee.

      Short description, for the stores, the website and the Cradle form

      NakGas lets you order LPG cooking gas from licensed local dealers in two taps. The KPDN controlled price comes first, then one small platform fee. You see the dealer’s name and truck before they arrive. Pay online or cash on delivery.

      NakGas membolehkan anda memesan gas memasak LPG daripada pengedar tempatan berlesen dengan dua ketikan. Harga kawalan KPDN dipaparkan dahulu, diikuti satu fi platform yang kecil. Anda tahu nama pengedar dan nombor lori sebelum mereka tiba. Bayar dalam talian atau tunai semasa penghantaran.

      Audiences: four people

      Illustrative people, written from the two calls and the audit. They are not research participants: each is tested against real users in the beta.

      Nadia, 34

      Working mother, 21st floor of a condominium in Johor Bahru

      Need
      Gas delivered to her unit at a time she chooses, without calling from the office.
      Fear
      Running out with dinner on the stove. A stranger at the door. A surprise charge for the lift.
      What wins her
      A scheduled delivery, the dealer’s name and truck plate before arrival, every fee shown before she pays, a photo when it is delivered.
      In the app
      High-rise address (floor, lift, unit), scheduled order, status steps, photo proof.

      Pakcik Hamid, 67

      Retired, a terrace house in Skudai

      Need
      The dealer and the price he knows, in Malay, paying cash.
      Fear
      Apps that take money he cannot see. Being pushed to pay online. Small text.
      What wins him
      Bahasa Malaysia from the first screen, cash on delivery, two taps, and a reminder so he never has to remember. His daughter can set it up for him.
      In the app
      Language picker, cash on delivery, one-tap reorder, reorder reminder.

      Arun and Mei Ling, both 27

      A young couple renting an apartment in Iskandar Puteri

      Need
      A licensed dealer near a home they just moved into, and a price they can check.
      Fear
      Being overcharged because they are new. An unsafe cylinder or a leaking hose.
      What wins them
      The KPDN price on the first line, licensed dealers only, safety tips in Help, e-wallet payment, a referral reward.
      In the app
      KPDN-verified dealer, itemised bill, online payment, help and safety tips, referral link.

      Mr Lim, 52

      A licensed dealer with a shop and two lorries in Johor Bahru, delivering by phone today

      Need
      More orders on the routes his lorries already drive, without paying a cut.
      Fear
      A platform that takes commission. Drivers who will not use an app. Disputes over a delivery. Late payment.
      What wins him
      No commission (Navin, 1 Oct), orders only inside his radius, 45 seconds to accept or pass, photo proof that settles disputes, a settlement history he can read.
      In the app
      Online switch, the 45 second countdown, stock and radius, photo proof, wallet and settlements. Phase Two: a WhatsApp accept flow for drivers who will not use an app.

      Do

      • Name the law’s price as the law’s: “harga kawalan KPDN”.
      • Say what happens next, with the number: 45 saat, RM3.50, hari ke-28.
      • Use the words people use: tong gas, abang gas, tukar tong, isi semula.
      • Keep one language per sentence. The tagline is the one exception.
      • Write “NakGas” as one word with a capital N and a capital G in copy.

      Don’t

      • Claim “termurah” or “terbaik” (cheapest, best). The gas price is fixed by law.
      • Use emojis, exclamation marks or hype in the interface.
      • Mix languages inside a sentence, as in “Order you dah confirm”.
      • Show the dealer’s phone number. The app replaces the call.
      • Blame the dealer when an order is late. Say what we are doing about it.

      Every Malay string is checked by a native speaker on Navin’s side before the build freezes the copy.

      The name: NakGas

      “Nak gas” is what a household says the moment the cylinder runs out: I want gas, we need gas. The name is the moment of need, and the app is the answer. Six letters, two syllables. Set on Tue 20 Oct 2026.

      NakGas. I want gas.

      It is the moment of need

      Every document Navin has written says GasNow. Gas Sekarang (6 Oct) and Gaslah (12 Oct) said when. NakGas says what the household actually says when the cylinder dies: nak gas. The need is the name; the app is the answer.

      Our read

      It is short

      Six letters, two syllables, one word, easy to say and to remember. On 20 Oct Bazil wrote “change the name all to NakGas”.

      Fact: his instruction, 20 Oct

      It is very Malaysian

      “Nak” is the most everyday Malay word for want; nobody has to be taught it. On 1 Oct Navin asked for “something very Malaysian”.

      Fact: his ask, 1 OctOur read: the meaning

      GasNow is weak to own

      gasnow.com has been registered by someone else since 2010, and “Gas Now” is already an app name abroad (audit, finding F15).

      Fact: audit, 29 Sep 2026
