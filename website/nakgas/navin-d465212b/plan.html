The core flows, step by step

Six flows carry the product. Each is written as the steps a person actually takes. Where a step is not yet decided, it says so at the foot of the flow.

Flow 1 · customer app and the engine

Order and assignment

  1. The customer taps Order on Home, or one-tap reorders the last order.
  2. Picks new cylinder or swap, size (14 kg by default), quantity, now or a scheduled slot, and a saved address flagged landed or high-rise.
  3. Checkout shows the bill: the KPDN controlled price first, then delivery, then the platform and processing fee, then the total. One promo code or points.
  4. Pays online, or chooses cash on delivery. The order is confirmed.
  5. The engine finds the nearest dealer who is online, inside their delivery radius and holding stock of that brand and size, and offers the order with a 45 second countdown.
  6. Decline, or no answer in 45 seconds: the order goes to the next nearest. The customer sees “Finding your dealer”.
  7. Accepted: the status moves to Dealer assigned, with the dealer’s name and truck plate. Never a phone number.
  8. Four declines in a row: the order lands in Admin › Escalations and an admin assigns it by hand. The customer sees that the team has it.

Open When a scheduled order is offered (at booking, or ahead of the slot); the refund rule when an order paid online finds no dealer. Owner: Navin, with Brand Method.

Flow 2 · supplier app

Delivery and photo proof

  1. The accepted job shows the address (floor, lift and unit for high-rise), the items, new or swap, and cash or paid.
  2. A shop operator assigns a driver and a truck. A driver operator is the truck, and goes.
  3. The driver taps Out for delivery. The customer gets the push: “Abang gas sedang dalam perjalanan.” / “Your dealer is on the way.” Status is time based; no live map in Phase One.
  4. At the door: the cylinder handed over; on a swap, the empty collected.
  5. The driver takes the delivery photo. Delivered cannot be pressed without it.
  6. Delivered: the customer receives the photo and the receipt, then a prompt to rate with an optional comment.
  7. The order trail in Admin holds every offer, decline, time and the photo.

Fact: photo proof is why Navin kept the supplier app over a messaging flow, call of Mon 28 Sep.

Flow 3 · money

Cash on delivery and settlement

  1. The customer picks cash on delivery at checkout and sees the exact total, RM33.50 on the worked example.
  2. The dealer’s job reads “Collect cash: RM33.50”.
  3. At the door the dealer collects, marks Cash collected, takes the photo, then Delivered.
  4. On an online order the platform holds the payment, keeps the platform fee and credits the dealer’s wallet with the gas price and the delivery fee (RM30.00 on the example).
  5. On a cash order the dealer already holds all of it, so the platform fee is recorded as owed and set against the dealer’s online credits.
  6. Payouts go from the ledger to the dealer’s bank account with one approver; the dealer sees each one in Settlement history, Admin sees it in Finance.

Our read Steps 4 and 5 are how “no commission” meets cash on delivery: the dealer never pays the platform, but the customer’s fee collected in cash has to come back. Open The mechanism for a cash-only dealer, and how often payouts run. Owner: Navin, with Brand Method.

Flow 4 · website, admin, supplier app

Supplier onboarding and KPDN approval

  1. A dealer finds For dealers on the website, or downloads the supplier app.
  2. Fills the form: business name as on SSM, SSM number, KPDN licence number and expiry, the licence file, contact, area, shop or truck or both, brands and sizes.
  3. The application lands in Admin › Approvals as New, with its source.
  4. An admin checks the KPDN licence: number, expiry, and that the name matches the SSM record.
  5. Approved: the dealer gets an SMS invite, logs into the supplier app with the same number, and the role is set.
  6. In the app: bank account, drivers and trucks, stock by brand and size, delivery radius. Then the dealer can go online.
  7. The licence expiry stays tracked. Admin sees it flagged ahead of time; the dealer sees a warning in Documents.
  8. Rejected: the reason is recorded and sent to the dealer.

Open Whether KPDN offers a licence lookup or the check stays by document; what an expired licence blocks. Owner: Navin.

Flow 5 · customer app

Reorder reminder

  1. Each delivered order records its date.
  2. The reminder is a simple scheduled push on the customer’s own rhythm: every 28 to 30 days by default, or the interval they set.
  3. Ahead of the expected day a push arrives: “Gas anda mungkin hampir habis. Pesan sekarang?” / “Your gas may be running low. Order now?”
  4. Tapping it opens the last order prefilled: same size, quantity and address. Two taps to confirm.
  5. The customer can change the interval or switch reminders off in Settings.
  6. Phase Two: the predictive version, from order history and then the smart regulator’s real balance.

Open How many days ahead the push goes. Owner: Navin, a setting in Admin.

Flow 6 · both apps

Language choice

  1. First open: the picker, Bahasa Malaysia or English, before login.
  2. Every screen, button and push notification follows the choice.
  3. Switch any time in Settings, with no reinstall and no lost data.
  4. The supplier app works the same way.
  5. The admin portal is English only.
  6. The website carries the same two languages with a switch in the header.
  7. Phase Two adds Chinese and Tamil.

Fact: picker on first open and a switch in Settings, Bazil on the call of Thu 1 Oct; multilingual is in Phase One. Open Whether the SMS code text follows the language.

The bill and the fee decision

KPDN requires the LPG price on its own line, so the bill is unbundled: the controlled price first, then the choices. The fee is charged per order, not per cylinder. Which fee, RM3.00 or RM3.50, waits on Brand Method’s price audit.

Facts
  • Navin leans to option B. His framing: RM3.00 platform kept whole, 50 sen covers the gateway, about 17 sen absorbed. Call of Thu 1 Oct.
  • Gateway he found: Razorpay Curlec, RM99 one time, then about 2% on the highest channel. His figures, to confirm.
  • He does not want a cylinder to cost the customer near RM39 to RM40.
  • His test of the central-region rival: four bill lines, a RM1.50 processing fee, RM139.10 for his basket (contents not stated).
  • The minuted status: “Needs further discussion”, pending our price audit.
Our read
  • The gateway takes a share of the whole order while the fee is flat, so the more cylinders in one order, the less of the fee is kept (the two-cylinder row). The 2% is the highest channel; FPX may cost less, so these kept figures are a floor.
  • Cash orders carry no gateway cost, so the full fee is kept, provided it comes back from the dealer (Flow 3).
  • The price audit should set the fee against rivals’ totals on the same basket, not their fee lines alone, and settle the high-rise tier at the same time.
  • The fee is a setting in Admin, so the decision does not hold up the build. The prototype shows option B, labelled “Platform & processing fee” / “Fi platform & pemprosesan”.

Owner of the fee decision: Bazil (price audit), then Navin. Rival checkouts are compared in the Competitors tab.
