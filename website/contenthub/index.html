The five laws of a Max Clarity slide
1 · One fact per slideIf you're explaining two things, that's two slides. A scroller decides in 1 second — give them one idea to catch.
2 · Show it, don't just say itEvery fact gets a literal visual ≥45% of the card. Number → bar. Process → flow. Comparison → two boxes. Thing → giant emoji. The eye reads the picture before the words.
3 · 12-year-old languageShort words, short lines. Read it aloud — if you stumble or it needs a second pass, cut it down. Hemingway-level plain. No jargon unless the slide defines it.
4 · Emoji & icons earn their placeOne purposeful emoji or icon per idea — as a signpost, not confetti. ⊘ for blocked, ✓ for safe, 🔥 for hot, 📊 for data. It speeds understanding; it never decorates.
5 · Each slide pulls to the nextEnd on a tease ("next, why it matters"). The deck is a chain — no slide is a dead end until the CTA.
The clarity test — before any deck ships
Mute test: scroll the deck with the words blurred. Do the visuals alone tell the story? If not, the visuals are too small or too vague.
Stranger test: would someone who knows nothing about the topic understand each slide in one read? If they'd ask "what does this mean?" — rewrite it.
Thumb test: at phone size, is the headline readable and the visual obvious without zooming? Big or it doesn't ship.
Never: tiny visuals, two facts fighting on one slide, paragraphs of text, jargon with no explainer, decoration that doesn't teach, or a flat slide with no motion hook on the cover. Those are the signs a deck isn't Max Clarity yet.

Slide 1 earns everything5 to 8 word headline, one pattern-interrupt visual, one open curiosity gap. If slide 1 fails, the rest is never seen.
The free second chanceIf a viewer scrolls past without swiping, Instagram often re-serves the post starting at slide 2. Design slide 2 as a second cover.
Slide 3 is the checkpointSwipe-through to slide 3 is treated as a key ranking signal. The first two slides are written for the algorithm as much as the reader.
Saves are the strongest signalGive a concrete reason to save on the close. Saved posts keep earning reach for days, not hours.
Dwell time ranks youEvery extra second on a slide is a signal. Loops, reveals, and ambient motion slow the thumb. Static slides leak reach.
Open loops between slidesEvery footer tease sells the next swipe. No slide is allowed to feel like an ending until the close.

V01 · The law of the engine
a weak hook with perfect editing dies · a great hook with phone editing travels
Priority order, never inverted: Hook → Script → Format → Editing. A weak hook with perfect editing dies. A great hook with phone editing travels. Spend time in that order.
One idea, seven outputs: carousel → standalone posts → threads → short video → long video (if the short earns it) → blog → freebie. Nothing is ever made once.
North star: maximum value, minimum production time, engineered to travel. If a step doesn't serve one of those three, cut it.

V04 · Carousel-first doctrine
the pipeline — every arrow is a decision, never a mood
The carousel is the test bench. It is the cheapest version of any idea. Idea → carousel (this file) → if it beats the account average → posts & threads → film the video → blog. We never film an unproven script.
And vice versa: when an outlier video format is found first, adapt its hook + structure as a carousel skin the same week — the carousel result decides whether we film our own cut.
Double Down rule: anything of ours that outliers gets remade (OG → double downs) and cloned across nouns — swap the brand, city, number, persona. One winner is a family of winners.

SOP 06 · Writing VoiceSharp Wit: how every line should sound, whatever the format. Internal only. Never in capture.
The north star READ FIRST
Sound like the smartest, funniest friend who actually knows this stuff. Take a side. Say the quiet part out loud. Land the joke. Make them feel seen, then hand them an edge they can use today. If a line is safe, obvious, or forgettable, it is not finished.
The seven pillars
1 · PolarizingPick a side and say it plainly. Name a villain or a "everyone believes X, and they're wrong." Balanced and neutral is the number one reason good content gets no reach.
2 · Professional but wittyCredible enough to trust, loose enough to enjoy. The authority is real; the delivery winks. Never corporate, never clownish.
3 · SmartPerspective, not just information. Give an angle they haven't heard and an edge they can use today. Respect their intelligence, skip the 101.
4 · FunnyEarn the laugh with truth, not gimmicks. A dry aside, an absurd comparison, a well-timed understatement. If the joke doesn't serve the point, cut it.
5 · Snappy punchlinesShort sentences that hit. Set up, then land. One idea per line. End every section on the line they'll screenshot.
6 · Very relatableSay the thing they were already thinking but couldn't word. Use their situations, their frustrations, their inside jokes. "How did you know" is the goal.
7 · Addicting & entertainingOpen a loop early, pay it off late. Momentum over completeness. Every line earns the next, so they can't stop, then feel rewarded that they didn't.
Do
Take a real position by line 2. If nobody could disagree with it, it isn't a take, it's a fact sheet.
Write like you talk. Contractions, short lines, rhythm. Read it out loud, and if you stumble, rewrite the line.
Payoff first, proof second. Lead with the punchy claim, then back it. Nobody stays for the build-up, they stay for the hit.
Plant one screenshot line per piece. That single line is the ad for everything else.
Be specific. Real numbers, real names, real scenarios. Specific is funnier and more credible than general, every time.
Don't
Don't hedge. "Kind of," "arguably," "in many ways" kill the punch. Cut them and commit.
Don't explain the joke or the point twice. Trust them to get it the first time.
Don't be cruel to be edgy. Polarizing about ideas, never mean to people. Punch at the system, never at the reader.
Don't bury the lede in setup. If the first line isn't pulling its weight, delete it and start on line 2.
Don't sound like a brand. No "we're excited to," no press-release voice, no emoji standing in for a personality.
Flat → Sharp Wit
Flat: "AI tools are changing how developers work."
Sharp Wit: "Your $10 coding tool just sent someone a $750 bill. Welcome to the new pricing."
Flat: "It's important to build multiple income streams."
Sharp Wit: "A title can be cut by a budget. An asset can't. Own the asset, not the title."
Flat: "The healthcare system is facing challenges."
Sharp Wit: "5,000 doctor slots. 529 takers. The safe path just quietly stopped being safe."
Line-level rules
Vary sentence length. A long one to build, a short one to land. Two short punches beat one long explanation.
One idea per sentence. If an "and" is doing heavy lifting, it's two sentences.
Kill adverbs and qualifiers. "Very," "really," "quite" weaken the verb. Pick a stronger verb instead.
End on the strongest word. Rewrite so the last word of the line is the one that hits.
Cut the first 10% and the last 10%. The warm-up and the wind-down are almost always deletable.
