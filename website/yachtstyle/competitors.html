Yacht Style Arabia, Peer & Competitor Audit

The brief is a website that ranks with the best in the world in the yacht business, on its own domain, yachtstylearabia.com, for Arab readers, with Dubai as the home market. That claim is measured against three groups at once: the global yacht titles, the sites that set the standard for a yacht listing, and the Gulf and Arabic titles a reader in Dubai or Riyadh can open today. This audit covers 18 of them, read from their own live pages on Thursday 1 October 2026 wherever the site allowed it, starting with the market they all sell into, and asks one question: what must Yacht Style Arabia say, show and prove to be the first choice.

6 Oct · What it changes

The ranking stays. Three things move up the plan

  • INFERENCE A headline on the first screen of a phone. The incumbent shows none. A Gulf owner who opens Yacht Style Arabia on a phone sees a story before anything else, and no advertisement above it.
  • INFERENCE Arabic as text, at its own addresses. The incumbent’s Arabic is a picture and a flipbook. Written Arabic is the one thing it cannot match quickly, and it is what search engines and AI answers can read.
  • INFERENCE The page code every story carries. Description, article data, author, dates, the language pair and a sitemap that works. The incumbent has none of these on its story pages. It is the cheapest lead to take on day one.
  • INFERENCE Watch point. It already publishes an llms.txt, so being first in AI answers is not uncontested. Ours is edited by hand and lists the guides and data pages, not only the latest posts.
  • FACT Question 07 is answered in part. The client calls it the main competitor, so the stance is to compete. Still open: its role as publisher of the official Dubai and Qatar show magazines, by its own account, in the month of the November shows.

  • FACT The direct incumbent has no Arabic website. The World of Yachts & Boats has a Dubai office and says it publishes the official show magazines for the Dubai and Qatar boat shows, but its Arabic sits in print and a flipbook. The site text is English, with no listings, no data and no media kit. On 6 October the client named it the main competitor in the Gulf, and our re-read that day found the same.
  • FACT Arabic yacht stories come from lifestyle desks. Arrajol, from the largest Saudi publisher, is the most active Arabic source we found. Robb Report Arabia, published from Dubai, shows no yacht story dated after 18 January 2026.
  • FACT The global leaders are English only, and one is arriving. BOAT International, SuperYacht Times, Yachting World and Robb Report have no Arabic. Informa and BOAT International are named supporters of the first Red Sea Yacht Show in Jeddah, 14 to 17 October 2027.
  • FACT Brokers have Gulf offices and no Arabic pages. Burgess lists a Dubai office. Fraser lists Dubai, Kuwait and Qatar. Both sites are English, or English and French.
  • FACT The readers are on phones. Mobile carries 60% of web traffic in the UAE and 68% in Saudi Arabia (StatCounter, September 2026). On our own lab phone profile the median main content time across the 16 sites we could measure was 13.9 seconds.
  • FACT ITP had no Gulf yacht title before the deal. INFERENCE Yacht Style is the only yacht brand on ITP’s own brand list, so the Arabia edition does not displace a sister title. Arabian Business already runs separate English and Arabic sites inside the group.
The finding that matters. INFERENCE In this field, authority and web presence are decoupled. The title with the regional relationships has the weakest website, and the titles with the strongest websites have no Arabic. A true Arabic and English yacht site, fast on a phone, with a Gulf data layer and a clean advertiser front door, is not a crowded position. On the evidence of this set, nobody holds it.

10.2 · Method

How every observation was produced

  • Sites. Three research passes on 1 Oct 2026 read each site’s own pages (home, about, advertise, listings, subscription) with a desktop browser user agent, and public reports for the market. No form was submitted and no cookie banner accepted.
  • Captures. Playwright Chromium headless, 1440x900, desktop Chrome user agent, locale en-AE, timezone Asia/Dubai, JPEG, viewport only, 1 Oct 2026. Cookies declined where a decline control was offered, in the settings layer where that is the only place it is offered; never accepted. Pop-ups closed with their own close control. Bot checks and refusals were not bypassed. 16 of 18 sites captured; every image was opened and checked.
  • Speed. Our measurement, not Google PageSpeed: Playwright Chromium, 390x844 phone profile, 4x CPU slowdown, 150 ms latency, 1.6 Mbps down, one cold run per site from Kuala Lumpur on 1 Oct 2026, cookies neither accepted nor declined. Largest contentful paint and bytes transferred until five seconds after the load event, or after 90 seconds where the load event never arrived. Directional only: read it as a ranking. 16 of 18 sites measured.
  • The field. 71 market points from organisers, authorities, company pages and named reports, each with its date and status. The Dubai boat show dates, the Red Sea Yacht Show announcement and Yacht Style’s own description were opened a second time by us to check them.
  • Format. The ranking, the two scores, the snapshot cards, the full profiles and the honesty table follow the Brand Method competitor audit standard.
  • Not done. No traffic panels, no rate cards, no media kit files, no interviews with advertisers, readers or Heart Media’s team, and no review of print issues.
