The 60 second verdict

This is a refresh, not a teardown, by the client's own instruction: logo and identity kept, the narration kept, the critique aimed at completeness, never at the company. 01
The company before the site
Who FA Herbs actually is, and the six jobs a winning estate must do for them

An audit that starts with the site judges aesthetics. This one starts with the company, so every score that follows is a score against revenue. FA Herbs Sdn. Bhd., 199401035761 (old 321444-X), incorporated 26 October 1994 in Selangor, manufacturing at Bukit Badong, Ijok. A GMP contract manufacturer of herbal supplements, personal care and food products, inside Sedania's Sustainable Brands division since the 2023 acquisition, positioned by its GM as a premier OEM and ODM manufacturing facility. In his words from the 14 August call: "It's not about products."

The commercial model

Contract manufacturing, two lanes

Ten product lines from bar soap to jamu to pre and post natal. Two buyer routes named in the call: startups take ODM, ready formulas, their label, fast to market; established brands take OEM, their own specification. The parent's page counts 250+ clients across hospitality, MLM, SME and corporate.

The demand problem

Three people carry all sales

The whole commercial engine is one sales manager, one sales executive and the GM. Group marketing serves the sister companies. Mervyn's own diagnosis on the call: sales and marketing is "the weakest link in the operations right now", and the new financial year has him gathering quotations in a hurry.

The hidden asset

A roster most factories would frame

Tanamera, Amway, Tupperware, Eskayvie and twelve more names sit on the Portfolio page today, as alt text behind placeholder tiles. The factory built Tanamera's entire product line, a brand shipping to 30+ countries. The proof exists. The site declines to show it.

"That is the weakest weakest link in the operations right now." Mervyn Goh, GM, on sales and marketing, 14 Aug 2026.

Six things a winning FA Herbs estate has to do

Job 1

Pass the OEM buyer's proof check. A brand owner picking a manufacturer verifies certificates, clients and capacity before the first call. Today every one of those checks lands on a placeholder.

Job 2

Explain OEM vs ODM in one screen. The two lanes Mervyn described are the site's natural architecture, and no page currently draws the fork.

Job 3

Publish the numbers. MOQ, capacity, timeline, formulation count. Rivals half FA Herbs' size publish all four; FA Herbs publishes none.

Job 4

Convert to WhatsApp and enquiry. A WhatsApp line exists (012-365 3232). The capture around it is a bare form and an "I'm interested" button with no context.

Job 5

Be findable by search and by AI. The call named ChatGPT and Gemini alongside SEO as a requirement. An engine can only cite numbers a site actually states.

Job 6

Sell the factory, not products. The GM's own framing. The site must read as a manufacturing partner's credentials, not a product catalogue.

The rest of this audit scores the estate against these six jobs. Every chapter names the job it serves.

A brand owner shortlisting OEM partners, from first search to first message

Claim audit

One company, three founding dates

SSM incorporation says 1994. The site says founded 1996 and "24+ years", copy that dates itself to roughly 2019. The Tanamera story starts the siblings in 1995. The site says 100+ clients, the parent says 250+. Our Story claims ISO 9001:2008; the hidden scan is the 2015 standard. Any buyer who notices one conflict stops trusting the rest.

The single highest-value fix

Publish what already exists

This is the rare audit where the fix needs almost no new material. The client logos are on the server. The certificate scans are on the server, and the QA office holds whatever current versions replaced them. The factory photos are on the server. Name the certificates, show the current ones, switch the real logos on with permission, and the proof layer goes from blank to enviable without a photoshoot.

Technical SEO and AI findability
The fundamentals exist, the substance behind them does not

The 14 August call named ChatGPT and Gemini findability as a requirement; that is a content problem before it is a markup problem.

07
The content engines
Claims that conflict, a roster left unsold, and a blog full of lorem ipsum

Three content systems live on this domain. A claims layer that contradicts itself and its parent. A roster that would headline any competitor's site, stored as alt text. And a blog of 51 posts, most of them the theme's lorem ipsum demo content, live, indexed and submitted to Google in the sitemap since 2020.

The claims, in conflict

Pick one set of numbers

100+ clients on the site vs 250+ on the parent's page. 24+ years vs incorporation in 1994 vs "established 1996". ISO 9001:2008 in the copy vs a 9001:2015 scan on the certificates page. Even the homepage counter hedges, rendering "0 +" years until JavaScript arrives. One reconciliation session with the client settles all of it.

The roster, unsold

Names any rival would frame

Tanamera, Amway, Tupperware, 101 Hair Care, Eskayvie and eleven more sit in the portfolio alt text behind placeholder tiles. One of them, Eskayvie, is a live Brand Method SEO client: a real, verifiable overlap and the natural first permission to ask for. Named clients with permission, MOQ and a process diagram beat any slogan this market writes.

The blog, demo filler

51 posts, mostly lorem ipsum

"Ornare ultricies amet", "hello world", "this is just a single clean post": theme demo posts from the 2020 install, live at 200 and listed in the sitemap Google reads. Every one is a page telling crawlers this site was never finished. Purge or noindex the lot in an afternoon.

What no page states, anywhere: no MOQ, no capacity figure, no formulation count, no lead time, no process explainer, no case study. These are the exact fields NaViBiotech, Skinlab and Bionutricia lead with, and per the parent's own copy FA Herbs has the stronger reality behind every one of them. The engine is not broken; it was never installed.

The resolution
Rebuild faherbs.com as the lead engine, on the identity the client is keeping

The direction agreed on 14 August holds: refresh, not teardown. Logo and identity unchanged, the founding narration kept, wording touched up here and there, and the exercise concentrated on design, UX and where people go and click. The architecture that serves it: one domain, two lanes, matching the two buyers Mervyn named, with the proof layer published for real and WhatsApp-first capture on both lanes. SEO ships free with the build, as offered in the call, and the AI findability checklist rides on the same published facts.
