Master SOP

The single source of truth · every cut is chosen here, never improvised
Canon
One cut: Faceted Folio. One size: Tarot 70 × 120 mm. Two groups: Business Strategy and Brand Strategy.
Brand Strategy fans out into mini decks, each with its own colour, pattern and front and back. Business Strategy is one deck, sectioned by chapter. Everything below sets how a card is cut, sized, named and built, so every future card matches without a second judgement call.

1 · The cut · card anatomy

Every card is built from the same five zones, top to bottom. The art is the navigation, the text box carries both a teaching view and a live client view, and the footer is the collector strip.

ZoneWhat it holdsRule
Title barCard name plus numbered pipName in Space Grotesk 600. Pip carries the card number, diamond for Business, round for story decks.
Art windowEngine glyph, deck pattern, corner ticks, vignette16:9. One glyph per card, the deck pattern sits behind it. This is how a card is recognised across sixty plus cards.
Type lineTag and subtitle, set symbolTag reads Framework in the teaching view and the brand name in the client view. Set symbol marks the deck.
Text boxLead line, three steps, flavour questionTwo stacked views. Framework view teaches the model, client view shows it filled for the live brand. The View control swaps them.
Collector stripMethodas mark, deck name, copyright, sectionFooter line. In the gallery it also shows the suit code, for example BS · 00-1 · CORE.

The back is brand constant: the deck pattern, a framed medallion at centre that holds the card's own glyph, the METHODAS OS lockup, the line The Brand Operating System, and a foot of brandmethod.co, the back code and the BrandMethod copyright. Every back uses the same coin seal at centre as a fixed container, holding the card's own glyph at one consistent, normalised size, mono and flat, a solid piece with its inner detail knocked out so it always reads clearly. The container is what keeps the sizes equal, no glyph is ever scaled up to fill its own shape, so none can come out bigger than the rest. The centre always reads as a clear, deliberate stamp and stays plainer than the lit, colourful, detailed glyph on the front.

Glyph colour is a rule, not a choice. On the dark framework face every glyph is white, so it reads against the deep frame. On the light client face the glyph switches to the deck's own deepest tone, so a bronze deck shows a bronze glyph and a teal deck a teal one, never a borrowed colour. In the framework view the back is dark and every back medallion and pattern line runs white. In the client view the whole back lightens to a soft tint of the deck colour, and the medallion glyph, the pattern lines and the lockup text all switch to the deck's deepest tone, so the back reads as cleanly on light as the front does. Success vs Failure is the single split deck: its three success cards run green and its three failure cards run red, on both faces and on the back, so the meaning is never inverted.

The pattern comes from the centre. On every back the deck pattern radiates from, or sits concentric with, the centre medallion, so the eye is pulled to the glyph and the METHODAS lockup below it. A pattern whose focus floats off to one side is wrong and must be re-centred. The front and back of a deck share one geometry.

The front always leads, the back stays quiet. The front face carries the title, the engine glyph over the deck pattern, the full teaching and client text and the footer, so it is the busy, information rich side. On the front the engine glyph is large, in the deck colour, lit with its glow, carrying its full detail of outlines, fills, accent dots and arrows over the deck pattern, so it is the detailed, complex side. On the back the same glyph sits inside a fixed coin container at one consistent, normalised size. Two rules keep that size honest. First, the front only presence scale is never carried onto the back, so a back glyph can never balloon past the others. Second, the coin is a fixed container and the glyph is drawn to a set share of it, so every back glyph, whatever its shape, reads at the same size. The glyph itself is a flat solid piece: its body filled in one flat tone with its inner detail knocked out in a contrasting tone, a dark cut on the light fill in Framework and a light cut on the deep fill in Client, so multi part glyphs like a screen and a figure read clearly and never blur into unclear solid layers. A dimensional glyph like the cube is the exception: it reads from its face shading alone and carries no cut lines, so it never picks up dark edge strokes. A few purely linear glyphs, the journey path or the text lines, stay as clean lines. The front is lit, colourful and detailed, the back is a calm flat stamp. The front visual must always read as more complex than the back, never the same.

Every back must read, and no two should be twins. The pattern has to be visible against the deck colour, never so faint the centre looks empty, and each deck's geometry must be its own: a compass for Introduction, a blueprint grid for Check, a coordinate map for Position, sound waves for Voice, projection beams for Pitch, a journey arc for Story, concentric hexagons for Facts, contour rings for Description, an editorial layout for Verbal and Visual, tilted orbits for Essence, and a directional arrow for Success and Failure. If two backs look like the same drawing in different colours, one of them is changed.

The shape echoes the pattern. The little badge behind the card number, the step markers and the foot marker all take the deck's own shape, not a default diamond. Diamond decks stay diamond, the radial and orbit decks (Introduction, Voice, Pitch, Story, Description, Essence) use a circle, the ruled decks (Check, Position, Verbal and Visual) use a square, Facts uses the hexagon, and Success vs Failure uses the triangle to match its fork, pointing up on the three success cards and inverting to point down on the three failure cards, on the pip, the step markers and the foot marker, so a glance tells a win from a loss. Pip, steps and foot marker always match within a deck.

Success vs Failure reads in two rows. The deck is laid out as one full row of the three success cards in green, then one full row of the three failure cards in red, so a reader never has to hunt for which is which.

3 · The size

This deck is read and presented, not fast played, so legibility and premium presence outrank shuffling speed. The deck ships at Tarot. Strategic stays on file as the print economy alternate.

SizeDimensionsWins onPick
Strategic63 × 88 mm · ratio 5:7Cheapest print, easy handling, off the shelf sleeves, collectible feelAlternate
Tarot70 × 120 mm · ratio 11:19Roughly 30% more area, comfortable read, the engine becomes the hero, oracle and gift presenceCanon
The deciding question: is this deck mostly read, or mostly played? Methodas is read, set out in sessions and kept as a premium tool, so Tarot wins. The only fix Tarot needs is on text light cards, where the template centres the block and nudges the art larger, so nothing ever looks empty.

6 · How to design a new cut

Eight steps, in order, every time. Nothing is invented at the card level.

StepDo
1 · Place itPick the group and the mini deck from section 5.
2 · Inherit colourTake the deck colour token. Do not invent a new one.
3 · Inherit patternUse the deck pattern for both the art window and the back.
4 · Set the pip and set symbolDiamond, round or hex, whichever the deck uses.
5 · Draw the glyphOne engine glyph for the art window, on the brand stroke.
6 · Write both viewsFramework view teaches the model, client view fills it for the live brand. Lead line, three steps, one flavour question each.
7 · Assign the codeSuit prefix, card code, number and back code from section 4.
8 · Drop into All Final CutsPlace it in its deck section in chapter order.
The rule of law. No card ships unless its colour, pattern, pip, size and code trace back to this page. New decks are added here first, then built. The cards in All Final Cuts are the final cut of whatever is set on the Master.
